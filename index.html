Crows Can Remember Which Humans Feed Them

Crows track individual humans who provide food, rewarding friendly behavior while avoiding others. Memory can persist for years, influencing approach and avoidance strategies. Juveniles learn by observing adults’ interactions. This behavior reflects complex memory, social learning, and conditional decision-making. Recognition of benefactors increases survival and foraging efficiency. Crows adjust strategies based on prior experience and observation of competitors. This ability helps them thrive in human-dominated environments. Social memory contributes to urban adaptability and conflict mitigation. It also demonstrates associative learning and predictive behavior in birds. The behavior highlights advanced environmental awareness and individualized social recognition.
