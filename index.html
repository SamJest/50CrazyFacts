Narwhal Predators Are Surprisingly Few

Killer whales are the primary natural predator of narwhals, occasionally polar bears threaten calves near the surface, and humans hunt them historically. Arctic isolation reduces predator diversity. Narwhal social structure — tight pods, coordinated dives — mitigates predation risk. Large males with prominent tusks deter some attacks. Ice cover provides shelter and escape routes. Deep diving also reduces exposure. Predation pressure is acute but specialized. Evolution favored alertness, collective awareness, and evasive behavior. Survival is a balance of stealth, power, and intelligence. Social strategy offsets physical vulnerability. In an ecosystem with few predators, adaptation is hyper-specialized.
