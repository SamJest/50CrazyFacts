Quantum Decoherence: The Enemy of Qubits

Qubits lose information when interacting with their environment, a process called decoherence.

Even tiny interactions with electromagnetic fields, vibrations, or thermal energy collapse superposition states. Controlling decoherence is critical for error correction, long computations, and practical quantum devices. Techniques include ultra-low temperatures, shielding, and isolation.

Why This Matters

Minimizing decoherence extends computation times and accuracy.

Decoherence studies inform quantum device design, quantum sensing, and fundamental physics research.

Did You Know?

Some qubit platforms now operate near absolute zero to mitigate decoherence effects.
