Eros: Desire Before Time

It’s not Cupid yet—how did desire begin?

💥 Impact

Eros myths highlight the primal, uncontrollable nature of desire and attraction.

They encode early observations of human motivation and social behavior.

🤯 Did You Know?

Eros emerged from Chaos, embodying desire and procreative force before gods existed.

Before Aphrodite, there was Eros, the primordial god of love and procreative force. Myths depict him as emerging from Chaos, spontaneously generating desire that drives creation and life. Hesiod describes him as a force both subtle and overwhelming, an invisible agent shaping relationships, fertility, and cosmic balance. Eros is more abstract than the later mischievous Cupid, representing instinctual attraction and the unseen power of passion. Early myths dramatize how desire is a primal, uncontrollable force, influencing gods and mortals alike. Scholars argue these tales show an early understanding of human motivation and the irresistible nature of longing. Stories emphasize unpredictability, illustrating extremes of influence that love and attraction can exert on behavior. Rituals and symbolic gestures in archaic Greece may reflect attempts to harness or honor this cosmic drive. In essence, Eros represents the raw, chaotic energy that propels life forward, encoded poetically in myth.
