Why Global Cuisine Exists Because of Trade Winds

Spices didn’t travel alone. Winds shaped dinner. Weather flavored culture.

Global cuisine from around the world expanded because of ancient trade winds. Sailors relied on predictable monsoons. These routes moved pepper, cinnamon, and ideas. Coastal cuisines absorbed foreign methods. Inland dishes followed later. Food maps mirror wind maps. Taste followed climate physics.

Why This Matters

This matters because globalization began in kitchens. Cuisine reflects environmental forces. Food history is climate history.

It also reframes cultural exchange. Trade wasn’t abstract. It was edible and daily.

Did You Know?

Monsoon winds still affect Indian Ocean food trade.
