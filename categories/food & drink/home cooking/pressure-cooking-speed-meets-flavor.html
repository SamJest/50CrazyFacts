Pressure Cooking: Speed Meets Flavor

Home cooks can drastically cut cooking time without losing taste.

Pressure cookers trap steam, raising internal temperature above boiling. This accelerates protein tenderization, flavor extraction, and starch gelatinization. Dishes like stews, beans, and risottos cook faster while retaining moisture and nutrients. Modern electric models add convenience and safety features.

Why This Matters

It matters because pressure cooking makes complex meals achievable for busy schedules.

It also reduces energy use and can unlock flavors that slow cooking sometimes diminishes.

Did You Know?

Pressure cooking was invented in 1679 by French physicist Denis Papin.
