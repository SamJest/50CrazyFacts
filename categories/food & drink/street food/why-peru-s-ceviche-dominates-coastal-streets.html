Why Peru’s Ceviche Dominates Coastal Streets

Fish, lime, and spice make a coastal legend.

Ceviche is raw fish marinated in lime juice, often sold by street vendors along Peru’s coast. Dating back to pre-Columbian times, it evolved with citrus from Spanish colonists. Street vendors added chili, onions, and herbs for flavor and freshness. Portability and speed make it ideal for daily meals. Ceviche’s popularity grew through festivals, markets, and tourism. Street culture keeps preparation visible and interactive. Modern ceviche includes fusion ingredients and presentation styles.

Why This Matters

It matters because ceviche preserves coastal culinary traditions. Street food ensures local economies benefit.

Street ceviche highlights freshness, seasonality, and culinary education. Global awareness increased through tourism.

Did You Know?

Ceviche has been sold on Peruvian streets for centuries.
