The Origins of Fast Food: From Diners to Drive-Thrus

Fast food wasn’t always about burgers and fries—it started with quick diner meals in the 1920s.

The fast food industry began in the early 20th century with diners and lunch counters offering quick meals to busy urban workers. White Castle, founded in 1921, standardized burger production and cleanliness, setting the stage for mass fast food culture. Drive-thru concepts emerged later, catering to car culture and convenience. Fast food grew from necessity, reflecting societal shifts in work and transportation. Standardization, marketing, and speed became its core identity.

Why This Matters

It matters because it shows how societal needs shape food trends.

It also demonstrates how business innovation created global eating habits.

Did You Know?

The first White Castle restaurant opened in Wichita, Kansas, in 1921.
