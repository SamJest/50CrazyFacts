Drive-Thru Culture: Food at the Speed of Cars

Fast food drive-thrus revolutionized how America eats, merging convenience with car culture.

The drive-thru emerged in the mid-20th century, allowing customers to order and receive food without leaving their vehicles. It catered to post-war suburban expansion, automobile dependence, and time-saving demands. Today, drive-thrus account for a significant portion of fast food sales globally. The system influenced menu design, packaging, and speed of service. Drive-thrus transformed dining into a mobile, instant experience.

Why This Matters

It matters because it reflects societal reliance on convenience and transportation.

It also shows how architecture and process influence food consumption.

Did You Know?

The first drive-thru was opened by In-N-Out Burger in 1948.
