Record-Breaking Tail Flicks: Stability in Action

Tail flicks aren’t just for show—they’re record-setting.

💥 Impact

The record of tail flick frequency illustrates extreme specialization and the importance of precise motor control in survival.

It also provides a model for studying biomechanics in fast-moving vertebrates.

🤯 Did You Know?

Basilsiks can flick their tails up to 120 times per minute to maintain balance.

Studies measuring tail movement during rapid water sprints reveal that basilisks can flick their tails laterally up to 120 times per minute. This rapid, precise motion stabilizes the body during high-speed locomotion, preventing falls or accidental submersion. Tail flicks also help in steering, counterbalancing angular momentum, and making last-second course corrections to avoid obstacles. The combination of speed, amplitude, and timing is astonishing, rivaling high-performance robotic stabilizers in complexity. Even small variations in tail length or flexibility dramatically alter sprint efficiency. Researchers use high-speed videography to quantify these flicks, revealing one of the most extreme examples of tail-assisted locomotion in vertebrates. Tail mechanics are thus central to the basilisk’s “water-walking” prowess, demonstrating evolution’s attention to integrated design.
