Tarsiers’ Eyes Are Fixed in Their Sockets

Evolutionary trade-off.

Tarsiers’ enormous eyes cannot move, forcing head rotation for visual coverage. Fixed eyes maximize size for night vision but reduce eye mobility. This evolutionary compromise favors nocturnal hunting efficiency. Neck rotation compensates for the lack of ocular movement. Fixed eyes are unusual among mammals and illustrate specialization. It shapes behavior, from head-turning vigilance to ambush hunting. This adaptation exemplifies evolutionary trade-offs for survival.

Why This Matters

Fixed eyes prioritize night vision over mobility.

Head rotation compensates, allowing panoramic surveillance.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers’ eyes are so large they are immobile in their sockets.
