Tarsiers Use Scent Marking

Smelly messages.

Tarsiers mark territories with secretions from scent glands located on chest or genital areas. These chemical signals convey ownership, reproductive status, and individual identity. Scent marking complements ultrasonic calls for communication. It reduces physical confrontations between neighbors. Scent marking is especially important for solitary, territorial males. The behavior is an evolutionary adaptation to dense forest environments where visual signals are limited. Chemical communication ensures survival and reproduction efficiency. Tarsiers’ olfactory signals reinforce social structure without direct conflict.

Why This Matters

Scent marking minimizes fighting and maintains territory.

It provides reproductive and identity information discreetly.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers use scent glands to mark territory and communicate chemically.
