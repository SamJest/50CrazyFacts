Capybaras Are Expert Swimmers

Water is their natural playground.

Capybaras are semi-aquatic and can stay submerged for up to five minutes. Their slightly webbed feet, eyes positioned high on their head, and nostrils on top allow them to see and breathe while mostly submerged. They escape predators, cool off, and even socialize in water. Swimming is essential for survival, especially in the floodplains and wetlands they inhabit. Capybaras often sleep in shallow water to avoid threats on land. Their swimming skill also facilitates feeding on aquatic plants.

Why This Matters

Highlights adaptation to semi-aquatic environments.

It shows the importance of habitat specialization in animal survival.

Did You Know?

They can close their nostrils and ears underwater to stay hidden from predators.
