Capybaras Can Submerge to Escape Predators

Stealth is a survival skill.

Capybaras evade jaguars, caimans, and anacondas by diving into water and remaining motionless with just nostrils above the surface. This behavior reduces detection and allows quick escape if attacked. Juveniles quickly learn from adults. The ability to stay submerged for several minutes is vital in predator-rich wetlands. Such escape tactics are an essential survival adaptation for semi-aquatic rodents.

Why This Matters

Shows behavioral innovation to avoid predation.

It highlights the importance of water as a refuge for semi-aquatic mammals.

Did You Know?

They can remain underwater for up to five minutes to evade predators.

Source

Madagascar Primate Research Program (mprp.mg)
