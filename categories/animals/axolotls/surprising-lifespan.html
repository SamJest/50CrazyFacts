Surprising Lifespan

Not just short-lived.

Axolotls can live up to 15 years in captivity, longer than many amphibians. Humans care because longevity affects population management and experimental planning. Observations show reproductive capacity persists into late years. Longevity is influenced by water quality, diet, and genetics. Studying lifespan informs ecology and husbandry. Extended lifespan enables longitudinal regeneration studies. Captive longevity contrasts with reduced wild lifespan. Axolotls remain relatively healthy throughout life. Long-lived individuals provide stable research populations.

Why This Matters

Extended lifespan enhances scientific and conservation opportunities. Allows long-term observation of biology and behavior.

Insights inform husbandry practices and breeding programs. Longevity affects research design and ecological studies.

Did You Know?

Axolotls can live over a decade under proper care.

Source

Herpetological Review, herpreview.org
