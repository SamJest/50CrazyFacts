Axolotls Can Withstand Partial Amputation Multiple Times

Unlike most animals, axolotls can endure repeated limb loss without losing regenerative ability.

Researchers have amputated the same limb multiple times in axolotls, and each time it regrows perfectly. This ability is due to pluripotent cells forming blastemas at the amputation site. Each regeneration restores bone, muscle, nerve, and skin in flawless alignment. Repeated regeneration does not reduce quality or speed, although age may slightly slow the process. This feature is rare among vertebrates and showcases their extraordinary cellular programming. It’s why axolotls are considered the gold standard for studying regeneration. In the wild, it improves survival after predator attacks.

Why This Matters

The ability to regenerate repeatedly highlights resilience at both cellular and organismal levels.

It demonstrates the potential for unlocking repetitive regeneration mechanisms for medical applications.

Did You Know?

Some axolotls have survived 10 or more repeated limb amputations with full regrowth.

Source

Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology (frontiersin.org)
