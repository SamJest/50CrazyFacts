Axolotls Can Use Gills, Lungs, and Skin Simultaneously for Breathing

Why settle for one breathing method when you can have three at once?

Axolotls rely on external gills, rudimentary lungs, and skin diffusion to absorb oxygen. The combination allows survival in low-oxygen waters. They can adjust usage of each method depending on activity level and water conditions. This redundancy ensures resilience in fluctuating aquatic environments. Their multi-modal respiration is studied to understand evolutionary adaptations in amphibians. It’s a rare physiological strategy that maximizes survival chances.

Why This Matters

Provides insight into adaptive respiratory strategies.

It informs conservation and aquatic biology, as environmental oxygen fluctuations can be mitigated by their flexibility.

Did You Know?

They can switch seamlessly between gill, lung, and skin respiration depending on need.
