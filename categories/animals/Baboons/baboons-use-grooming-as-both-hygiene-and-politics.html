Baboons Use Grooming as Both Hygiene and Politics

They aren’t just clean—they’re strategic.

Grooming serves multiple functions: cleaning fur, removing parasites, and maintaining social bonds. High-ranking baboons receive more grooming, reinforcing dominance and alliances. Grooming can also reduce tension after conflicts or encourage cooperation. Subordinate individuals use grooming to curry favor or avoid aggression. This social currency helps stabilize troop dynamics. Grooming sessions can last minutes or hours depending on the relationship.

Why This Matters

Highlights the interplay of biology and social behavior.

It shows that behavior can serve both practical and political purposes in primates.

Did You Know?

Baboons groom selectively, sometimes spending hours with a single ally to strengthen bonds.
