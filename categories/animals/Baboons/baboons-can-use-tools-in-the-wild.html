Baboons Can Use Tools in the Wild

Beyond hands, brains do the work.

Baboons have been observed using sticks to extract insects from holes or to dig for water. They can modify simple objects for specific tasks, demonstrating problem-solving abilities. Tool use is more common in troops living near humans or unusual environments. Juveniles learn through imitation, indicating social learning. These behaviors suggest advanced cognitive abilities relative to other non-human primates. Tool use enhances access to otherwise unavailable food sources.

Why This Matters

Shows cognitive sophistication and environmental adaptation.

It provides insights into the evolution of intelligence and culture in primates.

Did You Know?

They have used stones to crack open nuts in the wild.
