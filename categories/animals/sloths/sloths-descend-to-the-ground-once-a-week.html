Sloths Descend to the Ground Once a Week

The Great Descent.

Sloths leave the trees roughly once a week to defecate, despite increased predation risk. They dig shallow holes and cover their feces afterward. This behavior is critical for communication, as feces attract sloth moths that complete a symbiotic life cycle. Scientists speculate that nutrient cycling in the soil also benefits the forest. This weekly ritual is dangerous but vital for reproduction and ecosystem relationships. Predators like jaguars or ocelots can exploit this vulnerability. The behavior balances risk with ecological and biological needs.

Why This Matters

Weekly ground visits serve ecological and reproductive purposes.

It demonstrates the complexity of sloth survival strategies.

Did You Know?

Sloths risk predators by descending from trees to defecate once a week.
