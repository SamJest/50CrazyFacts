Sloths Have Unique Algae Camouflage

Walking forests.

Algae growing on sloth fur gives them a greenish tint that camouflages them in rainforest canopies. This symbiotic relationship provides protection from predators such as harpy eagles. Algae growth depends on sloth movement and humidity. It can also provide minor nutrition when ingested while grooming. Fur micro-ecosystems enhance the camouflage effect, including moths and fungi. This slow-growth camouflage is a passive yet vital survival tool. Camouflage is so effective that predators often overlook sloths entirely.

Why This Matters

Algae camouflage increases predator evasion.

It reduces energy needs for escape or fighting.

Did You Know?

Sloths often appear green due to algae growing on their fur, blending perfectly with leaves.
