Sloths Host Moths in Their Fur

Symbiotic hitchhikers.

Sloth fur often contains tiny moths that complete part of their lifecycle there. The moths feed on algae, helping it grow and maintaining camouflage. In return, the sloth’s fur provides shelter. This unusual symbiosis affects nutrient cycles and even reproduction for both species. The moth population can indicate sloth health and habitat quality. It’s a rare example of multi-level ecological relationships. This relationship is another reason sloths’ slow lifestyle is essential.

Why This Matters

Sloth-moth symbiosis promotes algae growth.

It creates a small but important micro-ecosystem.

Did You Know?

Sloths’ fur often hosts moths that help cultivate algae for camouflage.
