Sloths Can Hold Their Breath Underwater for Up to 40 Minutes

Submerged superpowers.

💥 Impact

Underwater endurance allows survival in flooded or riverine habitats.

It shows that lethargy extends beyond trees into aquatic adaptability.

🤯 Did You Know?

Sloths can hold their breath underwater for up to 40 minutes.

Sloths are exceptional swimmers, able to hold their breath for up to 40 minutes underwater in slow, controlled motion. Juveniles instinctively float and paddle to mimic adults. Observers note that aquatic excursions are rare but essential for crossing rivers or escaping predators. Evolutionarily, buoyancy and oxygen efficiency provide survival advantages. Their lungs and slow metabolism allow oxygen conservation, enabling prolonged submersion. Dense fur traps air, adding flotation and insulation. Even submerged, sloths maintain deliberate movements to avoid energy waste. Waterborne foraging or accidental immersion is handled without panic, demonstrating controlled adaptation. This aquatic skill is a hidden facet of their survival toolkit. Slow motion extends even underwater.

Source

Journal of Experimental Biology
