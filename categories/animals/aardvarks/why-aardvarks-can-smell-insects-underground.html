Why Aardvarks Can Smell Insects Underground

They pause. They sniff. Then they dig perfectly. It is not luck.

Aardvarks rely on an extreme olfactory system that detects termites through dense soil. Studies on how aardvarks locate underground termites in dry savannas show scent receptors outperform vision entirely. Their nostrils can seal shut to block dust while sampling air. Pressure differences in tunnels help funnel odor molecules upward. A secondary semantic variant appears in subterranean insect detection abilities of mammals. The brain filters irrelevant smells rapidly. This prevents wasted digging and conserves energy. Humans care because this sensory system outperforms many instruments. It forces a rethink of how smell can function.

Why This Matters

The finding matters for understanding animal perception limits. It shows that smell can be spatial, not just chemical. Aardvarks expand the known range of mammalian senses.

Engineers study this to improve underground search tools. Disaster rescue and landmine detection benefit from similar principles. Biology offers tested solutions.

Did You Know?

Aardvarks can detect termite nests buried more than one meter deep. Accuracy rates are unusually high.

Source

Journal of Experimental Biology
