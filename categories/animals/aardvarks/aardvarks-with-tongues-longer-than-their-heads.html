Aardvarks with Tongues Longer Than Their Heads

Some aardvarks have tongues surpassing 30 cm, a functional marvel.

💥 Impact

Their tongue adaptation illustrates extreme evolutionary solutions to ecological problems.

It highlights how morphology can reach functional extremes without compromising survival.

🤯 Did You Know?

Genetic studies suggest tongue length is highly heritable, a key factor in ecological success.

While most small mammals struggle to reach prey, aardvarks evolved tongues longer than their skulls. The elongated structure is covered in sticky saliva, allowing rapid termite capture without damaging mounds. Such tongue lengths are rare in mammals, especially relative to body size, making aardvarks extreme outliers. Evolution selected for length, flexibility, and muscular control simultaneously—a rare combination. The tongue’s extension and retraction mechanics allow feeding efficiency that would otherwise require larger jaw muscles or longer forelimbs. Observations show some individuals can flick their tongues up to 150 times per minute, extracting tens of thousands of termites in a single night. These anatomical extremes reflect intense evolutionary pressures for dietary specialization and nocturnal efficiency. The tongue is a living, multipurpose tool: sensory organ, feeding apparatus, and ecological instrument. The rare combination of extreme length, high speed, and adhesion makes this one of the most specialized mammalian tongues in existence.
