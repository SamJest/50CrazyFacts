The Name That Tops the Dictionary

Aardvark literally means “earth pig”—but its story is stranger than the name.

💥 Impact

Names matter because they shape perception. By labeling it “earth pig,” early colonists reduced a highly specialized termite hunter to a simple novelty, delaying serious scientific inquiry. The misnaming also shows how language can distort biological understanding. Yet the quirky name helped ensure its enduring place in popular culture, making it arguably the most famous obscure mammal.

Examining how aardvarks got their name illuminates broader issues of taxonomy, colonial influence, and cultural interpretation. It demonstrates that science is not just about discovery, but also about storytelling, misinterpretation, and myth-making.

🤯 Did You Know?

Despite the “pig” label, aardvarks are not related to pigs at all; DNA places them closer to elephants and manatees.

Aardvark comes from Afrikaans, literally translating to “earth pig,” yet Europeans only recorded the animal during Dutch colonization in the 17th century. Early explorers described it with bafflement: “half pig, half anteater, half nightmare,” since its nocturnal antics and long snout defied familiar categories. Indigenous peoples had their own names for it, often tying it to the underworld or fertility due to its burrowing nature. The word “aardvark” appeared in print as early as 1685, sparking debates among naturalists about its proper classification. It was first misclassified alongside pigs and bears before taxonomists assigned it its own family, Orycteropodidae. The literal alphabetic dominance of its name led to its accidental fame in dictionaries worldwide, but its ecological contributions remained overlooked. Even today, the name evokes a chuckle while masking a complex evolutionary narrative spanning tens of millions of years. The story of nomenclature illustrates the collision of colonial linguistics with indigenous knowledge and natural history. From a linguistic lens, it highlights how humans try—and often fail—to tame nature with words.
