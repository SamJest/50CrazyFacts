Historical records show aardvark burrows were used as natural boundary markers in precolonial African societies. Studies on aardvarks influencing land demarcation practices reveal how visible burrow systems defined territory. A secondary semantic variant involves animals shaping political geography. Burrows were stable, long-lasting, and easy to recognize. Communities trusted them more than temporary markers. This gave aardvarks an unintended political role. Humans care because governance often emerges from ecology. Nature structured authority.
