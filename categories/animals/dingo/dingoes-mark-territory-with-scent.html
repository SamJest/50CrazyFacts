Dingoes Mark Territory With Scent

Power of pee.

Dingoes use urine and feces to mark territory boundaries. Scent marking communicates presence, reproductive status, and dominance. They frequently patrol borders, renewing scent marks to deter intruders. Juveniles learn this behavior through observation. Territory size can vary from 5 to 100 square kilometers depending on prey abundance. Scent marking reduces physical confrontations with rival packs. The chemical signals carry detailed information about identity and health. Territoriality ensures resource access and reduces conflict.

Why This Matters

Scent marking maintains social structure and resource control.

It prevents unnecessary conflict and supports pack cohesion.

Did You Know?

Did you know dingoes use urine and feces to mark their territory boundaries.
