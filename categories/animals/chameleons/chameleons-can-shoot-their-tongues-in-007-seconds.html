Chameleons Can Shoot Their Tongues in 0.07 Seconds

Blindingly fast strikes that defy expectation.

💥 Impact

Tongue projection speed maximizes hunting success with minimal energy use.

It demonstrates the extreme biomechanical optimization of small predators.

🤯 Did You Know?

Chameleons can extend their tongues at blinding speeds to capture prey.

Chameleons project their tongues with explosive speed, sometimes in as little as 0.07 seconds, making the motion almost imperceptible to the human eye. This is achieved through a combination of elastic recoil from collagenous sheaths and rapid muscle contraction, storing potential energy that releases like a tensioned spring. Observers in Madagascar documented insects being snatched before the prey had any chance to react, which baffled early naturalists. High-speed videography later confirmed the extreme velocity and precision. Evolution has turned the tongue into both a projectile and a sticky trap, optimizing energy use while maximizing capture success. The coordination of eye tracking, head stabilization, and tongue projection is a marvel of neuromuscular integration. Watching it in action is akin to seeing a living crossbow fire with perfect timing and accuracy. Such rapid strikes underscore the extreme predatory specialization in small arboreal reptiles. The tongue’s mechanics are a masterclass in natural engineering.

Source

Proceedings of the Royal Society B
