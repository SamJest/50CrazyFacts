Pangolins Have Extraordinary Tongues

The tongue is a feeding superpower.

💥 Impact

Extraordinary tongues enable survival in insect-rich but challenging habitats.

It also highlights evolutionary investment in single-organ efficiency for specialist feeders.

🤯 Did You Know?

Pangolins’ tongues are sometimes longer than their entire body when uncoiled.

A pangolin’s tongue can extend 40 centimeters or more in some species, anchored to the sternum rather than the jaw. This allows deep probing into termite mounds and ant nests unreachable by claws alone. The tongue is coated in sticky saliva and muscular tissue that retracts rapidly. Tiny hooks along the surface trap insects efficiently. Unlike primates or anteaters, pangolins rely almost exclusively on this organ to gather food. Calves learn to manipulate the tongue gradually. Damage or loss of the tongue can be fatal, demonstrating extreme dietary specialization. Evolution has fused tongue length, stickiness, and musculature into an integrated hunting tool. The tongue also aids in self-grooming and removing parasites. Despite seeming delicate, it operates with remarkable speed and resilience. Nature engineered a precision tool for insectivory unmatched in mammals.

Source

Journal of Mammalogy
