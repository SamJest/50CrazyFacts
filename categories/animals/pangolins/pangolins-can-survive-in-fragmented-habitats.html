Pangolins Can Survive in Fragmented Habitats

Adaptation to patchy forests.

💥 Impact

Ecological flexibility allows pangolins to persist despite human-altered landscapes.

It also underlines the importance of habitat connectivity for long-term population viability.

🤯 Did You Know?

Pangolins have been observed crossing roads and open fields at night to move between forest patches.

Pangolins show remarkable resilience in fragmented landscapes, often crossing open areas at night to reach isolated forest patches. They rely on scent trails, camouflage, and nocturnal activity to avoid predators and humans. Semi-arboreal species use trees as bridges over gaps. Fragmented habitats increase energy expenditure and exposure risk but also force behavioral flexibility. Studies show home ranges adjust according to resource availability, indicating cognitive mapping skills. Reproduction may be affected in highly fragmented zones due to mate scarcity. Conservation corridors improve connectivity and gene flow. Pangolins’ survival demonstrates behavioral plasticity under anthropogenic pressure. They highlight the importance of habitat mosaics in tropical forest conservation. Adaptation under stress illustrates resilience in specialist species.
