Pangolins Are Masters of Insect Farming

Pangolins eat ants and termites exclusively. But how efficient are they at insect hunting?

💥 Impact

Pangolins control insect populations naturally, reducing ecological imbalance. Their diet maintains forest soil quality indirectly.

Hunting technique shows evolutionary trade-offs: efficiency over versatility. They sacrificed omnivory for unparalleled insect specialization.

🤯 Did You Know?

Pangolins’ tongues can be longer than their bodies relative to size, making them extreme feeders.

Pangolins have evolved an extreme form of myrmecophagy (ant-eating). Their long, sticky tongues can extend up to 40% of their body length, probing deep into termite mounds and ant nests. They consume thousands of insects per day without chewing; saliva and stomach keratin plates pulverize their prey. Some species can flick their tongue over 160 times per minute. Their olfactory sense pinpoints colonies buried meters underground. This specialization is so refined that pangolins can target specific ant species to maximize nutrient intake. Seasonal variations in insect abundance influence their foraging range. They also help regulate insect populations in forest ecosystems. Nature turned pangolins into precision insect harvesters. Survival is a balance of stealth, anatomy, and timing.
