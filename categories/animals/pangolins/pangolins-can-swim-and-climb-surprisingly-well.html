Pangolins Can Swim and Climb Surprisingly Well

Despite armor, pangolins aren’t just ground-bound — they’re versatile climbers and swimmers.

💥 Impact

Pangolin adaptability allows them to exploit diverse habitats despite being heavily armored. Flexibility equals survival advantage.

It demonstrates that extreme specialization doesn’t preclude mobility. Armor and movement can coexist.

🤯 Did You Know?

Pangolins have been observed swimming across rivers up to 100 meters and climbing 10-meter trees for feeding or refuge.

Pangolins are excellent climbers, using their curved claws to scale trees in search of ants, termites, or refuge. Their prehensile tails provide balance, acting like a fifth limb. Some species are semi-arboreal, spending significant time in the canopy. Surprisingly, pangolins are also capable swimmers, using limbs and tail coordination to paddle efficiently. Observations report pangolins crossing rivers or flooded areas without panic. The scales help reduce drag in water and provide slight buoyancy. Arboreal skills allow escape from terrestrial predators, while swimming expands foraging range. Evolution produced a small mammal with exceptional versatility, combining armor with mobility. Their stealth is enhanced by multi-terrain capability.
