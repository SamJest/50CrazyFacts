Okapids Were Unknown to Science Until 1901

Despite being large mammals, okapis were scientifically described only in the 20th century.

💥 Impact

Delayed recognition underscores how habitat inaccessibility can shield species from global awareness.

It also reminds us that “discovery” is often a Western-centric perspective ignoring Indigenous knowledge.

🤯 Did You Know?

Okapis were first scientifically documented less than 120 years ago despite being common locally.

Native to dense Congo rainforests, okapis remained virtually invisible to European explorers. The first scientifically recognized specimen was obtained in 1901 by Sir Harry Johnston. Indigenous knowledge existed for centuries, but the West knew little. Misidentification as a forest giraffe or zebra delayed recognition. Early photographs and specimen transport were challenging due to dense undergrowth and tropical diseases. The species was declared a new genus, Okapia, highlighting its uniqueness. Zoological illustration followed. Specimens became centerpieces in European museums, generating curiosity. Discovery blended adventure, luck, and local expertise. Science lagged behind culture and geography.
