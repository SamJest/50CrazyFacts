Okapids’ Daily Activity Peaks at Dawn and Dusk

Crepuscular patterns govern survival.

💥 Impact

Activity timing balances feeding efficiency, predator avoidance, and energy conservation.

It also highlights the intersection of physiology, behavior, and ecosystem context.

🤯 Did You Know?

Okapis spend 60–70% of daylight hours resting and feed mainly during dawn and dusk.

Okapis are most active during early morning and late afternoon. Dense forest floors trap heat and humidity, so cooler periods reduce thermal stress. Feeding, traveling, and social interactions align with these crepuscular windows. Predation risk is minimized as cats and other large predators follow different activity patterns. Activity monitoring shows short bursts of movement followed by resting, conserving energy. This schedule harmonizes feeding needs with environmental pressures. Light conditions complement visual and acoustic senses. Social signals are also optimized when other species are less active. Evolution favors rhythm synchronization with environmental cues. Behavioral ecology demonstrates time-based survival strategies.

Source

Journal of Wildlife Management
