Okapids’ Olfactory Communication Is Understudied

They rely heavily on smell.

💥 Impact

Scent marking shapes social structure and reduces aggressive encounters.

It also suggests that human perception underestimates non-visual communication.

🤯 Did You Know?

Footprint-associated scent marking allows okapis to map territories invisibly.

Okapis use scent glands on their feet and faces to mark territory, convey reproductive status, and signal stress. Chemical cues persist in the humid forest, guiding conspecifics along invisible social maps. Mothers communicate subtly with calves via scent trails. Dominant males deposit markers at boundaries to avoid confrontations. Rain washes away or intensifies chemical signals, influencing behavior seasonally. The chemical complexity rivals pheromones in insects in some functional aspects. Olfactory communication complements acoustic and visual cues, forming a three-pronged signaling system. Human observers often miss this vital sense. Evolution prioritizes discrete, long-lasting communication over loud, risky calls. Forest navigation and social spacing depend heavily on smell.

Source

Journal of Chemical Ecology
