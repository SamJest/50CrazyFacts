Okapids’ Tongues Are Surprisingly Long and Prehensile

Tongues for stealthy browsing.

💥 Impact

Prehensile tongues optimize energy intake and reduce injury from thorns.

It also demonstrates evolution’s capacity for multi-functional anatomical solutions.

🤯 Did You Know?

Okapis can lick their own eyes and ears with their tongue to remove debris.

Okapis have tongues up to 18 inches long, prehensile enough to strip leaves, twigs, and buds from branches while avoiding thorns. The tongue wraps around foliage, retracting quickly into the mouth. Darkly pigmented tips prevent sunburn during daytime feeding. Calves practice with short protrusions, learning dexterity over months. Tongue agility allows selective feeding, avoiding toxic plants while maximizing nutrition. Evolution favors efficiency in dense forests where every bite counts. Observations reveal that okapis can groom ears, eyes, and even nose with the same tongue. The tongue’s multitasking mirrors primate dexterity in miniature. This adaptation merges feeding, hygiene, and social behavior. Subtle anatomy shapes daily survival.
