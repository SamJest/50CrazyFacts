Aye-Aye’s Foraging Sequence Is Algorithmic

Their tapping, listening, gnawing, and probing follow a repeatable, stepwise algorithm.

💥 Impact

It’s significant because it shows that natural behaviors can resemble engineered processes.

It also illustrates that evolution can create complex sequences comparable to human-designed algorithms.

🤯 Did You Know?

Aye-ayes repeat the sequence hundreds of times per night with high precision.

Observation and video analysis reveal that aye-ayes follow a remarkably consistent sequence during foraging. They start by scanning visually, then tap, listen, gnaw, and finally extract larvae. Each step triggers the next in a feedback loop informed by auditory and tactile input. The process minimizes error and energy expenditure. Behavioral studies suggest that the sequence is encoded in neural circuits, almost like a biological algorithm. Variations occur only due to local wood conditions or prey size. This pattern demonstrates how complex, multi-step behaviors can evolve in non-human species. The “algorithm” ensures high success rates in locating hidden food. Researchers are exploring computational models inspired by this behavior.
