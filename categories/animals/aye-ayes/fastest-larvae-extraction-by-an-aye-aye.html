Fastest Larvae Extraction by an Aye-Aye

An individual extracted insect larvae in under 12 seconds, a documented extreme.

💥 Impact

Significance: Demonstrates the upper limit of natural foraging efficiency.

Exceptional extraction speed may influence survival success and reproductive advantage.

🤯 Did You Know?

This individual performed 5 extractions per minute without error.

In a controlled study, one aye-aye located, gnawed, and retrieved insect larvae in less than 12 seconds. Typical extraction takes 15–30 seconds. This speed requires coordination between finger, jaw, auditory, and tactile systems. It represents peak performance of their percussive foraging system. Fast extraction reduces energy expenditure and exposure to predators. High-speed extraction showcases the limits of neural and muscular coordination in small nocturnal primates. Such extremes are rare and provide benchmarks for comparative studies. The behavior reflects evolutionary pressure to maximize efficiency in competitive habitats. Researchers use these records to understand the interplay between anatomy, neural control, and sensory perception. Extreme foraging speeds may also influence territory dynamics and social hierarchy.

Source

[Animal Behaviour, sciencedirect.com]
