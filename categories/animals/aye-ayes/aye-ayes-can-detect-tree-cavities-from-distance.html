Aye-Ayes Can Detect Tree Cavities From Distance

They are expert locators of hidden meals.

By tapping and listening, aye-ayes can detect hollow cavities in tree branches and trunks containing insect larvae before even reaching the spot. This allows precise targeting and energy-efficient foraging. It reduces unnecessary gnawing and effort. This ability requires auditory sensitivity, memory, and precise finger coordination. It’s a prime example of how sensory integration supports survival.

Why This Matters

Shows advanced environmental perception in a nocturnal primate.

It emphasizes the importance of multi-sensory processing in efficient foraging.

Did You Know?

They can detect hollow spaces inside wood several centimeters away before tapping.

Source

Madagascar Biodiversity Center (mbc.mg)
