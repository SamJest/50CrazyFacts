The Teeth of a Vampire-Primate

Aye-ayes’ continuously growing incisors resemble rodent teeth, not typical primate dentition.
💥 Impact
›

Aye-ayes’ teeth demonstrate the importance of anatomical specialization in survival. They allow access to food resources unavailable to competitors, which directly affects ecological balance. This trait also offers insight into primate evolution and diet specialization.

Teeth like these also fascinate dentists and evolutionary biologists, as they illustrate natural solutions to wear and tear. They provide a live model of dental growth control.

🤯 Did You Know?
›

Aye-ayes can gnaw through branches up to 2 cm thick to reach larvae. Their teeth can grow several millimeters per month.

Unlike most primates, aye-ayes have incisors that never stop growing. This feature is convergent with rodents rather than lemurs. They use these teeth to gnaw through bark and access insect larvae hidden deep inside wood. The incisors are self-sharpening due to their chewing action. This adaptation allows survival in habitats where other food is seasonal or hidden. The dental structure also influenced early misclassification as a rodent. Evolution favored this trait because it lets the aye-aye exploit a very narrow ecological niche. Their teeth are large relative to skull size, giving them a comically exaggerated appearance. Maintaining these teeth requires constant feeding on hard wood. Failure to gnaw properly can lead to dental overgrowth and malnutrition.
