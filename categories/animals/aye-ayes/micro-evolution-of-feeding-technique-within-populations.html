Micro-Evolution of Feeding Technique Within Populations

Aye-ayes show regional variations in tapping style adapted to wood density.
💥 Impact ›

Significance: Shows that evolution can fine-tune behavior to microhabitats.

It illustrates that behavioral plasticity can complement anatomical specialization.

🤯 Did You Know? ›

Aye-ayes in dense forests tap 20–30% slower than in sparse forests.

Research shows that aye-ayes in different Madagascar forests adjust tapping force and rhythm based on local tree species. In areas with denser wood, tapping is slower and stronger; in softer wood, faster and lighter. These micro-evolutionary adaptations occur within populations over relatively short timescales. Cultural transmission may also play a role, as young aye-ayes observe adults. This demonstrates that even mechanical behaviors can adapt to environmental variation. The interplay between anatomy, sensory perception, and learning creates flexible but precise feeding mechanics. Such subtle behavioral evolution is rare in primates. It shows that even a seemingly fixed “instinct” can be locally tuned. Understanding this highlights the responsiveness of species to ecological conditions.
