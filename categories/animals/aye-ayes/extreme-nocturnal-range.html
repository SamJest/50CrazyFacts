Extreme Nocturnal Range

Some aye-ayes travel over 2 km per night, exceptional for their size.

💥 Impact

Significance: Shows the upper limits of locomotor ability and territory exploitation in nocturnal primates.

Long-range foraging illustrates the species’ adaptability under ecological stress.

🤯 Did You Know?

Aye-ayes can travel 2.2 km in a single night when food is scarce.

While most small primates stay within a home range of 0.5–1 km nightly, some aye-ayes traverse 2 km to forage. This extreme roaming likely occurs in areas with sparse prey or high competition. Covering such distances requires energy-efficient locomotion, precise navigation, and spatial memory. Tail and limb adaptations assist with balance during long tree-to-tree leaps. This behavior is rare among nocturnal lemurs and demonstrates the species’ capacity for endurance. It also highlights behavioral flexibility in response to environmental constraints. Researchers track these ranges using GPS collars, revealing surprising mobility. Long-distance nightly movement may influence seed dispersal, predator exposure, and social interactions. Extreme ranges inform conservation planning regarding habitat corridors.
