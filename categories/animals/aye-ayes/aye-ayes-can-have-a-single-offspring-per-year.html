Aye-Ayes Can Have a Single Offspring Per Year

Reproduction is slow and deliberate.

Aye-ayes typically give birth to one offspring per year, reflecting low reproductive rates. The infant depends entirely on the mother for several months, requiring careful energy investment. This strategy favors survival of the individual rather than large litter numbers. Low reproductive output makes populations vulnerable to threats like habitat loss or hunting. Conservation plans must consider slow population growth in protection strategies.

Why This Matters

Shows the vulnerability of slow-reproducing species to environmental pressures.

It emphasizes the need for long-term conservation planning to maintain populations.

Did You Know?

They have one baby at a time, with long maternal care extending over six months.

Source

Madagascar Primate Research Program (mprp.mg)
