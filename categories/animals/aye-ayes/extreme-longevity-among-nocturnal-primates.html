Extreme Longevity Among Nocturnal Primates

Some aye-ayes quietly defy the clock.

💥 Impact

Their longevity allows cumulative learning and ecological influence.

It emphasizes how life-history traits shape ecological roles and evolutionary outcomes.

🤯 Did You Know?

Aye-ayes can live up to 25 years in captivity and 15–20 years in the wild, unusually long for nocturnal primates.

In the wild, aye-ayes can live 15–20 years, while in captivity they reach 25 years. For a nocturnal, solitary primate, this longevity is striking. Their slow metabolism, nocturnal lifestyle, and specialized diet contribute to extended life, giving them decades to refine foraging skills, learn territory layout, and avoid predators. Longevity also allows for prolonged parental investment: mothers teach offspring complex tapping and extraction techniques. Such lifespan is unusual among similar-sized primates, and it underscores how ecological niche and behavioral adaptations interact with life-history strategies. Over decades, a single aye-aye can influence insect populations, forest ecology, and even gene flow within fragmented populations. Evolution seems to favor endurance alongside specialization. This lifespan is another layer in the portrait of a truly unique primate.

Source

American Journal of Primatology
