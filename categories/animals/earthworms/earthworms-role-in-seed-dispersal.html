Earthworms’ Role in Seed Dispersal

Soil taxis for plants.

💥 Impact

Earthworms contribute to plant reproduction and distribution through seed dispersal.

It highlights a mutualistic relationship between invertebrates and vegetation.

🤯 Did You Know?

Earthworms help disperse seeds by consuming and depositing them with nutrient-rich castings.

Earthworms aid in dispersing seeds by ingesting small seeds that survive gut passage and are deposited with nutrient-rich castings. Juveniles observe adult feeding patterns and follow similar paths, ensuring continued soil seed redistribution. Observing their feeding shows selective ingestion, movement, and deposition patterns that influence plant community structure. Fossil evidence suggests ancient earthworm activity may have shaped plant colonization patterns. Intelligence manifests in soil selection, feeding choices, and burrow navigation that benefits both worm and plant. This ecological service supports forest regeneration, agricultural seedling survival, and microhabitat creation. Even minor worm movements can shift local plant diversity. They are tiny gardeners of the soil.
