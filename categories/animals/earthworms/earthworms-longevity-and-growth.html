Earthworms’ Longevity and Growth

Earthworms can grow old underground.

💥 Impact

Earthworm longevity enhances ecosystem engineering and population resilience.

It highlights the ecological significance of lifespan variation in small invertebrates.

🤯 Did You Know?

Earthworms can live several years, influencing soil health and ecosystem stability.

Earthworm lifespans vary by species, ranging from 4 to 8 years, with some surviving over a decade in optimal conditions. Juveniles grow rapidly when following adults’ burrow systems with abundant nutrient access. Observing population dynamics shows longevity is linked to soil quality, moisture, and predator avoidance. Fossil studies suggest long-lived earthworms have shaped soil structure consistently for millions of years. Intelligence manifests in burrow selection, feeding efficiency, and environmental adaptation that maximize lifespan. Longer-lived individuals contribute disproportionately to population stability and soil engineering. Even subtle changes in habitat quality can affect growth rates and longevity. They are silent long-term architects of ecosystems.

Source
