Aye-Ayes Can Survive in Small, Isolated Forest Patches

They are resilient yet vulnerable.

Aye-ayes can persist in small forest fragments if sufficient food and trees exist. They navigate fragmented landscapes using canopy bridges or by carefully moving on lower branches. Survival in isolated patches demonstrates behavioral and ecological flexibility. However, population size is limited, making genetic diversity a concern. Conservation strategies often focus on connecting fragments to maintain viable populations.

Why This Matters

Shows resilience to habitat fragmentation but emphasizes limitations.

It highlights the importance of landscape connectivity in conservation planning.

Did You Know?

They can survive in isolated patches for years if food and canopy cover remain sufficient.
