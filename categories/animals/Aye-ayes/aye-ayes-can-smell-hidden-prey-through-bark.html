Aye-Ayes Can Smell Hidden Prey Through Bark

Chemical cues guide their hunting.

Aye-ayes use their sense of smell to detect insect larvae beneath bark before tapping. This olfactory information helps them determine where to focus their percussive foraging. Smell works together with touch and hearing to optimize prey detection. Their nasal sensitivity allows them to pick up subtle differences in scent, such as pheromones or decaying wood odors. It’s a critical adaptation for a diet that relies on concealed, high-protein food.

Why This Matters

Shows the integration of multiple senses in foraging efficiency.

It underscores the complexity of predator-prey interactions in nocturnal ecosystems.

Did You Know?

They can detect insect larvae hidden up to several centimeters inside bark using scent.

Madagascar Biodiversity Center (mbc.mg)
