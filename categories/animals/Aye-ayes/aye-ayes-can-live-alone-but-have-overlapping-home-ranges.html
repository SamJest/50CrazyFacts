Aye-Ayes Can Live Alone But Have Overlapping Home Ranges

They might be solitary, but they’re not entirely isolated.

Aye-ayes are primarily solitary, yet their territories often overlap with others. They maintain boundaries using scent marking, vocalizations, and subtle behaviors. Overlapping ranges allow access to multiple feeding sites while minimizing direct conflict. This balance between solitude and overlap helps maintain genetic diversity and ensures food resources are efficiently used. Researchers use GPS tracking to study how these ranges shift with season, age, and population density. Solitary yet socially aware, aye-ayes have a unique approach to coexistence.

Why This Matters

Highlights the complexity of solitary animal social structures.

It provides insight into how territoriality and resource sharing coexist in nocturnal species.

Did You Know?

They can tolerate neighboring aye-ayes in overlapping ranges without aggressive conflict.

Source

Madagascar Primate Research Program (mprp.mg)
