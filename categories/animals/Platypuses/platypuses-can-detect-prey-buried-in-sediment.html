Platypuses Can Detect Prey Buried in Sediment

Underwater detective.

Using their sensitive bills, platypuses can sense invertebrates buried under riverbed sand or mud. Electroreceptors detect muscle movement, while tactile receptors sense texture. They dig or probe to extract prey like insect larvae or crustaceans. This allows them to access food invisible to sighted predators. Juveniles learn the technique gradually. It is a critical skill in rivers with dense sediment or flowing debris. Such foraging behavior shows exceptional sensory integration.

Why This Matters

Sediment detection allows exploitation of hidden food resources.

It reduces competition with other predators relying on surface prey.

Did You Know?

Platypuses can locate and extract prey buried under river sediment.
