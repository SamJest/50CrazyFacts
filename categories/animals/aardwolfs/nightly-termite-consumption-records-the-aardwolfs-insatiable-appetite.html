Nightly Termite Consumption Records: The Aardwolf’s Insatiable Appetite

One small mammal can eat 300,000 termites in a single night.

💥 Impact

Aardwolfs exemplify extreme dietary efficiency in small mammals.

It highlights the surprising scale at which a tiny insectivore can operate.

🤯 Did You Know?

Aardwolfs can consume enough termites to outweigh themselves several times over in a single night.

Aardwolfs hold one of the most extreme feeding records for mammals their size. Observations show a single individual can consume 300,000 termites in just one night, relying on rapid tongue flicks, precise paw movements, and optimized foraging paths. This staggering intake provides nearly all required calories, highlighting the efficiency of their specialized digestive system. The number varies depending on termite density, mound size, and season, but even minimal counts exceed what most predators of comparable size would need from larger prey. Extreme consumption reflects both morphological adaptations (tubulidentate teeth, sticky tongue) and behavioral mastery (patterned nightly foraging). It’s an evolutionary outlier: few mammals sustain survival on such tiny prey in such volumes. These records fascinate ecologists because they show the extremes of specialization and efficiency. Understanding the mechanics behind these numbers illustrates how evolution can produce extraordinary performance without brute force.
