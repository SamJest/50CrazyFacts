Bees Are Critical for 1 in 3 Bites of Food

Their work underpins global agriculture.

💥 Impact

Bees’ role in food security underscores their ecological and economic importance, far beyond honey production.

It demonstrates the cascading impact of a keystone species on global nutrition and agriculture.

🤯 Did You Know?

Bees are essential pollinators responsible for approximately one-third of the food we eat.

Bees pollinate an estimated 70 of the 100 crop species that feed 90% of the world, affecting about a third of human calories. Without their labor, fruit, vegetable, and nut production would plummet, dramatically altering diets and economies. Honeybees are particularly efficient because they forage systematically, visiting flowers in consistent sequences that maximize pollination. They also prefer high-reward flowers, indirectly shaping plant evolution and productivity. Historically, civilizations depended on wild pollinators, but domestication amplified agricultural yield and reliability. Declines in bee populations due to pesticides, habitat loss, and disease pose real threats to global food security. The economic value of pollination services runs into hundreds of billions annually, showing that tiny insects drive massive systems. Bees’ ecological work illustrates the interconnectedness of species and the hidden engines behind human survival. Every apple, almond, or blueberry on your table owes its existence to these miniature powerhouses.

Source

Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)
