Bees Can Perform Arithmetic-Like Tasks

Counting with tiny brains.

💥 Impact

Honeybees’ numerical competence challenges assumptions about cognition being brain-size dependent.

It offers insights into the evolution of abstract problem-solving across species.

🤯 Did You Know?

Honeybees can perform simple arithmetic tasks like addition and subtraction using visual cues.

Honeybees can perform simple arithmetic tasks, such as addition and subtraction, using visual cues associated with rewards. In controlled experiments, bees were trained to associate colors or symbols with numerical changes, then tested for their ability to select correct answers to problems like "2+1" or "3–1." Success rates exceeded chance, indicating genuine cognitive processing rather than rote memorization. This demonstrates that bees possess numerical competence previously thought exclusive to vertebrates. Neural circuits in the mushroom bodies handle these tasks, showing convergence of learning structures across phyla. Historically, the notion of arithmetic in insects seemed absurd, but rigorous experiments confirm this unexpected intelligence. Arithmetic-like skills allow bees to optimize foraging and evaluate quantity-based resources, enhancing energy efficiency. The behavior underscores that complex cognition does not require a large brain. Studying such abilities in insects informs both neuroscience and AI models.
