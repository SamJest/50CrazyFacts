Sloths Have a Unique Jaw Structure for Leaf Diet

Leaf-crunching tool.

Sloths’ jaws are elongated with sharp ridges to efficiently chew tough leaves. The teeth are peg-like, lacking enamel, but continuously grow to compensate for wear. Slow chewing and prolonged digestion maximize nutrient extraction. Muscles and jaw mechanics are adapted for minimal energy use. They can grind leaves without extensive movement, conserving calories. This adaptation aligns with their low-energy lifestyle. The jaw design is a key factor in surviving on fibrous foliage.

Why This Matters

Jaw adaptation allows survival on low-calorie food.

It reduces energy expenditure in feeding.

Did You Know?

Sloths’ peg-like teeth and specialized jaws let them chew tough leaves efficiently.
