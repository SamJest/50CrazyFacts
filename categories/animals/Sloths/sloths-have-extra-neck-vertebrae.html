Sloths Have Extra Neck Vertebrae

More neck than most mammals.

Most mammals have seven neck vertebrae, but three-toed sloths can have nine, allowing them to rotate their heads 270 degrees. This extreme flexibility compensates for their limited mobility. It helps them spot predators or find food without moving their bodies. Two-toed sloths have six or seven neck vertebrae, slightly less than their three-toed cousins. Extra vertebrae develop during embryonic growth and are a key evolutionary adaptation. This trait is rare in mammals and remarkable for arboreal life.

Why This Matters

Neck flexibility enhances vigilance without energy expenditure.

It allows better predator awareness and resource acquisition.

Did You Know?

Three-toed sloths can rotate their heads almost completely around due to extra vertebrae.
