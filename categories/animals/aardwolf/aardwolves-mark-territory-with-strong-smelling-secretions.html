Aardwolves Mark Territory with Strong-Smelling Secretions

Territory talks. Scent communicates. Friends and foes know the rules.

Aardwolves use anal gland secretions to mark territory. These pungent scents deter intruders and signal reproductive status. Marks are left on grass, termite mounds, and rocks. Scent marking reduces direct confrontations.

Why This Matters

The fact matters because chemical communication prevents fights. Efficient survival strategy.

It also influences mating success. Proper territory control ensures access to food and partners.

Did You Know?

Did you know an aardwolf can identify neighbors versus strangers using scent alone.
