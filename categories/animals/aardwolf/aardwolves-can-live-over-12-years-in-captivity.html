Aardwolves Can Live Over 12 Years in Captivity

Long lifespan surprises scientists. Bugs sustain life. Care matters.

Aardwolves in captivity can live up to 12–15 years, longer than many wild counterparts. Controlled diets, absence of predators, and veterinary care extend life. This contrasts with average wild lifespans of 6–8 years.

Why This Matters

The fact matters because longevity studies inform conservation strategies. Health management matters.

It also shows how human care can aid research into insectivorous carnivores. Captivity offers observation opportunities.

Did You Know?

Did you know captive aardwolves remain active at night just like their wild counterparts.
