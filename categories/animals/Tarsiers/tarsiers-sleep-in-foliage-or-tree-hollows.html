Tarsiers Sleep in Foliage or Tree Hollows

Safe sleeping.

Tarsiers rest in dense foliage, tree hollows, or vine tangles during daylight hours. Sleeping sites provide camouflage from predators like snakes, owls, and civets. They often curl tails around their bodies for added protection. Choice of sleeping location is influenced by safety, temperature, and proximity to feeding areas. Secure roosts allow uninterrupted rest necessary for nocturnal activity. Sleeping behavior demonstrates a combination of behavioral and physical adaptations. It ensures energy conservation and predator avoidance. Tarsiers’ sleep strategies highlight nocturnal specialization.

Why This Matters

Safe sleeping sites increase survival.

They provide rest for nocturnal hunting and predator evasion.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers sleep in foliage, tree hollows, or tangled vines to avoid predators.
