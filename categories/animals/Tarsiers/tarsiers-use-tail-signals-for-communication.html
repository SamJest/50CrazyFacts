Tarsiers Use Tail Signals for Communication

Tail language.

Tarsiers have long tails that serve as visual signals during social interactions. Tail movement indicates alertness, aggression, or submission. In dim light, subtle tail flicks convey important messages without vocalization. This helps maintain territory boundaries and reduce conflict. Tail communication complements ultrasonic calls and scent marking. Such multimodal signaling is rare among small primates. It demonstrates behavioral complexity in a nocturnal species.

Why This Matters

Tail signals reduce physical confrontations.

They enhance social awareness and territory management.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers use tail movements to communicate status and mood to others.

Source

Journal of Mammalogy (mammalogy.org)
