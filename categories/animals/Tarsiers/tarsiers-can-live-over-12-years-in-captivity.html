Tarsiers Can Live Over 12 Years in Captivity

Long-lived for their size.

Tarsiers typically live 10–12 years in the wild but can exceed 15 years in well-managed captivity. Lifespan depends on diet, stress, and environmental enrichment. Captive tarsiers require complex arboreal setups and proper nocturnal conditions. Longer life in captivity allows researchers to study behavior, reproduction, and physiology in detail. Longevity data is essential for conservation planning and species management. Understanding lifespan helps predict population trends in fragmented habitats. Providing enriched habitats ensures welfare and survival in captivity.

Why This Matters

Longer lifespan allows reproductive contribution over many years.

It aids conservation programs and long-term research.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers can live up to 15 years or more in captivity with proper care.

Source

International Union for Conservation of Nature (iucn.org)
