Aardvarks Have Poor Eyesight but Excellent Low-Light Adaptation

Their eyes aren’t sharp, but they’re perfectly suited. Night vision isn’t about detail. It’s about sensitivity.

Aardvarks have relatively poor visual acuity. However, their eyes are adapted to low-light conditions. Rod cells dominate their retinas. This allows movement detection in darkness. Vision supports navigation rather than fine detail. Other senses fill the gaps.

Why This Matters

It matters because specialization beats generalization. Aardvark senses are balanced for nocturnal life.

This explains their calm movement in near-total darkness. They don’t need clarity, just contrast.

Did You Know?

Aardvarks rely more on smell and hearing than sight when foraging. Vision plays a secondary role.
