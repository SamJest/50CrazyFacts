Aardvarks Can Smell Insects Buried Over a Meter Underground

Aardvarks hunt in near-total darkness. They don’t rely on sight at all. Instead, they follow an invisible trail beneath their feet.

Aardvarks have an extraordinarily powerful sense of smell that allows them to detect ants and termites buried more than a meter underground. Their long snouts house highly developed olfactory structures specialized for scent tracking. While foraging, an aardvark slowly moves across the ground, sniffing constantly. Once it detects a colony, it begins digging with precision. This ability lets aardvarks locate food sources that are completely hidden from other predators. It also reduces competition, since few animals can access these insects so efficiently.

Why This Matters

This matters because it makes aardvarks highly specialized insect predators. Their success depends on sensory ability rather than speed or strength.

It also explains why aardvarks struggle when habitats are altered. Disrupting soil and insect populations removes their primary food signals.

Did You Know?

Aardvarks can identify whether a termite mound is active or abandoned purely by smell. They rarely waste energy digging into empty nests.
