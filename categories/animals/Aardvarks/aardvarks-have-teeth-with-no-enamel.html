Aardvarks Have Teeth With No Enamel

Aardvark teeth are unlike almost any other mammal’s. They don’t shine or sharpen the way you’d expect. In fact, they break most dental rules.

Aardvark teeth lack enamel entirely and are instead made of thousands of thin tubules of dentine. These tubules run vertically through the tooth, making it flexible and resistant to wear. Because aardvarks eat insects rather than chewing tough material, enamel isn’t necessary. Their teeth also grow continuously, compensating for constant abrasion. This unusual structure is unique among living mammals.

Why This Matters

It matters because tooth structure reveals diet and evolution. Aardvarks adapted perfectly to their narrow feeding niche.

This uniqueness also helps scientists identify aardvark fossils. Their teeth are instantly recognizable in the fossil record.

Did You Know?

If an aardvark loses part of a tooth, it doesn’t suffer the same way other mammals would. The tubule structure reduces cracking and damage.
