Aardvarks Adjust Their Activity Based on Temperature

Aardvarks don’t follow a strict clock. They follow the weather. Heat decides when they move.

In cooler seasons, aardvarks may emerge earlier in the evening or even become partially active during daylight. In extreme heat, they stay underground longer to conserve energy. This flexibility helps regulate body temperature. It also reduces water loss in dry environments. Their schedule shifts with climate conditions.

Why This Matters

It matters because climate change directly affects aardvark behavior. Altered temperatures can disrupt feeding routines.

This adaptability helps them survive variable environments but has limits. Extreme shifts can still be dangerous.

Did You Know?

In some drought years, aardvarks have been observed foraging near dawn instead of at night. This is unusual but increasingly common.
