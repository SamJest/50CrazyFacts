Aardvarks Can Change Burrow Locations Frequently

Aardvarks don’t get too comfortable. Home is temporary. Mobility keeps them safe.

Aardvarks often dig new burrows rather than reusing the same one. This reduces parasite load and predator predictability. Burrow placement shifts with food availability. Some burrows are used only once. This constant movement increases survival.

Why This Matters

It matters because burrow availability affects many other species. Frequent relocation creates new shelters across landscapes.

It also makes aardvark populations harder to monitor. They leave little permanent trace.

Did You Know?

An aardvark may dig dozens of burrows within its home range in a year. Only a few are used long-term.
