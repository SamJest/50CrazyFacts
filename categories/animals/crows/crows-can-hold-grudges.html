Crows Can Hold Grudges

Your past mistakes are remembered.

💥 Impact

Crows’ grudge-holding illustrates complex social memory and strategy.

It also warns humans that interactions have long-term effects.

🤯 Did You Know?

Crows can remember humans who wronged them and act accordingly for years.

Crows not only recognize individual humans but also remember negative experiences and can hold grudges for years. Experiments where humans captured or threatened crows led to targeted alarm calling, avoidance, and mobbing behaviors. Juveniles observe these reactions, learning to recognize and avoid specific threats. Studies show that crows may even recruit allies, coordinating group responses against a perceived human danger. This behavior demonstrates memory, social learning, and strategic planning. Fossil records suggest long-term social memory may have deep evolutionary roots in corvids. The implication is that humans interacting carelessly with wildlife can face long-lasting consequences. Observing a crow reacting aggressively to a past threat is both eerie and fascinating. These birds transform social memory into practical survival strategies.
