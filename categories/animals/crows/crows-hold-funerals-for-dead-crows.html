Crows Hold Funerals for Dead Crows

Social rituals among birds.

💥 Impact

It matters because it provides evidence that crows comprehend mortality in a way that informs survival strategies.

It also highlights complex social structures in birds beyond simple flocking behavior.

🤯 Did You Know?

Some crows repeatedly revisit the site days later.

Crows gather around deceased individuals, sometimes for hours, producing loud calls and inspecting the body. These events, termed “funerals,” allow crows to learn about dangers in the environment. The gatherings are often attended by family or community members. Some birds appear to guard the body or warn others repeatedly. Juveniles observe these events, acquiring survival knowledge. Behavior includes touching or pecking at the corpse. Funeral-like responses may enhance group cohesion and vigilance. They are highly context-dependent and vary with social structure. Understanding these behaviors sheds light on mourning and death recognition in non-human animals. It also suggests emotional complexity and cultural transmission of information. These events indicate advanced social awareness and communication.
