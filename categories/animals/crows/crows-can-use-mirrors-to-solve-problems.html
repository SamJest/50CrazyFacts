Crows Can Use Mirrors to Solve Problems

Reflective problem solvers.

💥 Impact

Crows’ mirror-based problem-solving indicates high cognitive flexibility.

It demonstrates abstraction, foresight, and non-human self-awareness.

🤯 Did You Know?

Crows have been observed using mirrors to retrieve otherwise inaccessible food.

Crows can understand reflections in mirrors, using them to locate hidden food or navigate obstacles. Experiments show they adjust movements based on mirrored images, indicating self-awareness and spatial reasoning. This behavior requires visual cognition, memory, and abstract thinking. Juveniles gradually acquire mirror skills through trial-and-error. Mirror use demonstrates understanding of indirect cues and problem-solving flexibility. Such cognition is rare among non-mammals. Birds combine mirror information with prior knowledge to make decisions. Understanding reflective problem-solving reveals parallels with primate intelligence. It also illustrates capacity for abstraction and advanced perception. Researchers use these tests to study reasoning and innovation.
