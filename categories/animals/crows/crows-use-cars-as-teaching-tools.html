Crows Use Cars as Teaching Tools

Adults show youngsters the ropes.

💥 Impact

Shows cultural transmission and teaching in birds.

It also emphasizes urban adaptability and intergenerational learning.

🤯 Did You Know?

Adult crows teach juveniles to use traffic for nut-cracking.

Some urban crows have been observed using roadways not only for nut-cracking but as a teaching platform for juveniles. Adult crows demonstrate dropping nuts in traffic, retreating to safe distances, and allowing juveniles to observe before attempting it themselves. This represents intergenerational knowledge transfer, a rare example of “teaching” outside humans and primates. Juveniles often mimic the adult steps almost perfectly, learning risk assessment, timing, and the mechanics of vehicle interaction. Researchers highlight that this is a culturally transmitted skill, passed from adult to juvenile over years. Observing this process is like watching a tiny feathered classroom in action. It’s an extraordinary example of problem-solving combined with social learning. Evolution favors individuals who can transmit knowledge efficiently, ensuring survival of the smartest and bravest. Crows blur the line between instinct and culture in the animal kingdom.
