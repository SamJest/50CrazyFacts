Crows Hold “Funerals”

When one dies, others gather and observe, perhaps to learn.

💥 Impact

Crow “funerals” reflect social learning and threat awareness.

It also demonstrates intergenerational knowledge transfer in wild bird communities.

🤯 Did You Know?

Crows hold gatherings around dead peers, likely to learn about threats and hazards.

Crows have been observed gathering around deceased crows in what looks like mourning rituals. These “funeral gatherings” can involve tens of birds, silent staring, and investigative behavior. Scientists suggest this isn’t grief in the human sense, but rather a way to learn about threats and dangers in the environment. By studying the corpse and observing reactions, crows gain knowledge about predators, hazards, or risky areas. Experiments with taxidermied crows demonstrated that other crows respond with alarm calls and gathering behavior even if the death was simulated. Juvenile crows often accompany adults, absorbing these social lessons about survival. This behavior highlights a level of social intelligence and communication rarely attributed to birds. The eerie coordination and apparent solemnity of these gatherings blur the line between instinct and cultural practice. It’s a reminder that intelligence manifests in many unexpected ways. Crows are not just clever—they are socially aware and strategic.

Source

Proceedings of the Royal Society B
