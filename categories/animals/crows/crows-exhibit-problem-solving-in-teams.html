Crows Exhibit Problem-Solving in Teams

Two heads are better than one.

💥 Impact

Cooperative problem-solving shows intelligence, social coordination, and planning.

It also highlights intergenerational learning and adaptability.

🤯 Did You Know?

Crows work in teams to solve complex problems and access resources.

Crows can work cooperatively to solve complex tasks, such as moving objects together to reach food or coordinating actions to access rewards. Juveniles learn team strategies from adults, passing knowledge culturally. Experiments demonstrate they adjust roles, communicate cues, and plan sequences collectively. This social problem-solving indicates advanced cognition, foresight, and flexibility. Fossil and behavioral evidence shows cooperative problem-solving has been selected over evolutionary time. Observing coordinated efforts in crows resembles watching a miniature, feathered engineering team. Cooperation is strategic, often enhancing efficiency and reducing individual risk. This ability underscores intelligence not just in individuals but in groups. Teamwork is coded into their survival strategies.
