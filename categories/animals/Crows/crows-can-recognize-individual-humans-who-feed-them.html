Crows Can Recognize Individual Humans Who Feed Them

Favors are remembered.

Crows remember which humans provide food and may interact positively with them in future encounters. They approach trusted humans while avoiding unfamiliar or threatening individuals. Such recognition improves access to resources and strengthens learned associations. Juveniles learn from adults about safe humans. This demonstrates long-term memory and social awareness.

Why This Matters

Crows’ recognition of benevolent humans shows adaptive learning.

It highlights how experience shapes interactions with other species.

Did You Know?

They may bring shiny objects as gifts to trusted feeders.
