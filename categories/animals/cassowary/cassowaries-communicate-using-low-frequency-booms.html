Cassowaries Communicate Using Low-Frequency Booms

Jungle subwoofers. Distance calling. Secret messaging.

Cassowaries produce deep, low-frequency booms that travel more than a kilometer through dense forest. These sounds help advertise territory, attract mates, and warn intruders without visual contact. Their throat anatomy allows resonation, producing frequencies humans can barely hear. Calls vary among individuals, and experts can identify birds by their boom patterns. Juveniles gradually develop these vocalizations. Low-frequency sound reduces energy cost for long-distance communication. It also conveys body size and dominance to rivals.

Why This Matters

Low-frequency communication is critical for social interaction and mating in dense rainforests.

It reduces the need for dangerous close encounters with rivals. This acoustic adaptation improves survival and reproductive success.

Did You Know?

Did you know cassowary booms can be felt as vibrations in the ground, like mini earthquakes.
