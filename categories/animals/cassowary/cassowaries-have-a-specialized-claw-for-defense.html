Cassowaries Have a Specialized Claw for Defense

Lethal weapon. Third toe terror. Jungle slashers.

Cassowaries have a dagger-like inner claw on each foot, up to 12 cm long. They use it to slash predators or rivals. Powerful leg muscles allow quick, accurate strikes capable of severe injury. The claw is curved and retractable while walking. Aggressive displays often include raising one foot to show the claw. Juveniles learn to wield it instinctively during play and defense practice. The claw is both a hunting deterrent and a survival tool. Human encounters with unaware individuals can be dangerous due to this adaptation.

Why This Matters

Claws provide effective defense against predators in dense forests.

It makes humans and animals alike cautious around cassowaries, underscoring their evolutionary armor.

Did You Know?

Did you know cassowaries’ inner claw is sharp enough to fatally injure a human if threatened.
