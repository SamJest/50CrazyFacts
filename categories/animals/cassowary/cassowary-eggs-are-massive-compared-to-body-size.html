Cassowary Eggs Are Massive Compared to Body Size

Gigantic eggs. Surprising proportions. Reptile-like.

Cassowary females lay eggs that can weigh up to 1.5 kg (3.3 lbs), enormous relative to their body size. These eggs are among the largest of any bird species, second only to ostriches. The thick shell protects developing chicks from predators. Males incubate eggs for 50–60 days, showcasing unusual avian parental care. Chicks are precocial and highly independent soon after hatching. Egg size also influences clutch number, typically 3–5 per season. Large eggs allow more nutrient storage, supporting initial chick growth.

Why This Matters

Massive eggs ensure chicks are well-developed and resilient at hatching.

Parental investment in incubation supports survival in dense, predator-rich rainforests.

Did You Know?

Did you know cassowary eggs are green-blue, blending with forest leaf litter for camouflage.
