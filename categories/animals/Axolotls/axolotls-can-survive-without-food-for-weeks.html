Axolotls Can Survive Without Food for Weeks

These amphibians have an impressive endurance for fasting.

Axolotls can slow their metabolism drastically to survive extended periods without food. In the wild, this helps them endure times when prey is scarce. They use stored energy in fat deposits and conserve energy by reducing movement. Even during fasting, regenerative processes continue at a slower pace. In captivity, they can survive several weeks without feeding, though gradual health decline occurs if prolonged. This adaptation ensures survival through environmental stressors like droughts or seasonal scarcity. Their ability to withstand starvation is another layer of resilience that makes them a fascinating study organism.

Why This Matters

Fasting endurance shows evolutionary strategies for surviving unstable environments.

It provides a model for studying metabolism, energy conservation, and stress physiology in vertebrates.

Did You Know?

They can survive up to 8 weeks without food under cool, oxygenated water conditions.

Source

International Union for Conservation of Nature (iucn.org)
