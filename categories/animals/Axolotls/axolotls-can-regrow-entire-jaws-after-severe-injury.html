Axolotls Can Regrow Entire Jaws After Severe Injury

Jaws, critical for feeding, are fully regenerable in axolotls.

Severe jaw injury triggers regrowth of bone, cartilage, and muscle with perfect alignment. This is coordinated with teeth and skin regeneration to restore full function. The jaw’s mobility and bite strength return completely, allowing normal feeding. It’s controlled by a precise cellular and molecular cascade involving blastema formation and stem-cell activation. Humans have no comparable natural ability. Observing jaw regrowth provides insights into craniofacial tissue engineering and repair. It’s a stunning example of evolution creating perfect repair mechanisms.

Why This Matters

Shows that even mechanically complex structures can be regenerated.

It informs regenerative medicine, reconstructive surgery research, and evolutionary biology.

Did You Know?

Regrown jaws can fully function in weeks after injury.

Source

Developmental Biology Journal (devbiol.org)
