Axolotls Can Sense and Avoid Harmful UV Light

Their skin and eyes detect ultraviolet light, helping them survive harsh sunlight.

UV detection allows axolotls to seek shade or deeper water, protecting their sensitive skin and gills. Excess UV exposure can damage DNA and reduce regeneration efficiency. Their behavioral adaptation minimizes risk while optimizing feeding and mating activity. This capability shows how sensory awareness combines with survival strategies. It’s particularly important in shallow lake environments exposed to intense sunlight. UV sensitivity complements photoreception in eyes and skin.

Why This Matters

Protects them from DNA damage and skin injury.

It illustrates evolutionary strategies for survival in fluctuating light environments.

Did You Know?

They can detect and respond to harmful UV exposure before visible damage occurs.

Source

Journal of Experimental Biology (jeb.biologists.org)
