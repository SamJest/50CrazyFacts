Axolotls Can Survive Low-Oxygen Environments

These amphibians are masters of hypoxia, tolerating oxygen levels that would cripple other creatures.

Axolotls can survive in water with very low dissolved oxygen due to efficient gills and skin respiration. They slow metabolism dramatically, entering a state similar to torpor. This allows them to survive during droughts or in stagnant waters where oxygen levels plummet. Their heart rate drops, and cellular activity slows to preserve energy. Scientists study this adaptation to understand hypoxia tolerance in vertebrates. Even under stress, axolotls can recover fully once oxygen levels normalize. This trait is critical for survival in their native lakes, which can fluctuate seasonally. Hypoxia tolerance is another layer of resilience that makes axolotls extraordinary in both natural and lab environments.

Why This Matters

It provides insight into survival strategies under environmental stress.

It also helps researchers design experiments for tissue regeneration under low-oxygen conditions.

Did You Know?

They can survive several hours in water with oxygen levels near zero.

Source

Journal of Experimental Biology (jeb.biologists.org)
