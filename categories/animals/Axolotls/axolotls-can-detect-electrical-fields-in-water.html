Axolotls Can Detect Electrical Fields in Water

Axolotls have a hidden superpower: they can sense the tiny electrical signals emitted by moving creatures.

Their skin and sensory organs pick up weak bioelectric fields produced by prey or other animals. This allows them to locate prey even in complete darkness or turbid water. The ability complements their vibration and chemical detection systems. Scientists study this to understand electroreception in vertebrates, which is rare among amphibians. It’s an elegant evolutionary adaptation for survival. Axolotls integrate electrical cues with lateral line vibrations and vision to navigate efficiently.

Why This Matters

This ability gives them a hunting edge in low-visibility habitats.

It also inspires bioelectronic sensors and underwater navigation technology.

Did You Know?

They can detect electrical signals as low as a few microvolts in water.

Source

Frontiers in Physiology (frontiersin.org)
