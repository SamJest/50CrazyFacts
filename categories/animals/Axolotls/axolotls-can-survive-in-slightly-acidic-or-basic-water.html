Axolotls Can Survive in Slightly Acidic or Basic Water

These creatures are surprisingly tolerant to pH changes that would harm other amphibians.

Axolotls can tolerate water with pH levels slightly outside the neutral range (around 6–8) without immediate harm. They regulate internal pH through ion transport and kidney function. This flexibility helps them survive in urban lakes with fluctuating chemical compositions. Extreme pH can damage skin and gills, but moderate changes are manageable. Understanding this tolerance helps aquarists maintain healthy tanks and informs environmental studies on habitat degradation. It also reveals the adaptability of amphibian physiology to changing conditions. Their pH resilience contributes to their survival in variable natural habitats.

Why This Matters

Shows adaptability in the face of environmental fluctuations.

It informs conservation and aquaculture strategies.

Did You Know?

They can survive short-term pH fluctuations of 1–2 units without noticeable stress.
