Axolotls Have More Chromosomes Than Humans—And It Shows!

If you think humans are genetically complex, think again. Axolotls have an astounding amount of DNA crammed into every cell.

An axolotl has 28 pairs of chromosomes—almost double the 23 pairs humans have. Their genome is about 10 times larger than ours, making it one of the biggest genomes sequenced in vertebrates. This massive genetic library contains all the information for their regenerative abilities, neoteny, and unique physiology. Scientists are still unraveling why so much DNA exists, including vast stretches that don’t code for proteins. Studying their genome helps biologists understand evolution, gene regulation, and regeneration. The sheer size of their genome is a reminder that complexity isn’t always visible at first glance. Decoding it is like reading a book the size of a small library, all within a single cell.

Why This Matters

Axolotl genetics illustrate that more DNA doesn’t always mean a more complex organism, but it can encode extraordinary biological capabilities.

It also provides a treasure trove of information for regenerative medicine and evolutionary biology.

Did You Know?

Their genome is roughly 32 billion base pairs, compared to humans’ 3 billion.
