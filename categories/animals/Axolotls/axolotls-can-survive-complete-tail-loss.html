Axolotls Can Survive Complete Tail Loss

Even losing their main propulsive organ isn’t the end—they regenerate fully.

If an axolotl loses its tail, all tissues—including muscle, nerves, bones, and skin—regrow with perfect functionality. Tail regeneration is faster in younger animals but remains effective throughout life. The process involves blastema formation, cellular dedifferentiation, and precise patterning. Functionality is fully restored, allowing swimming and balance to resume normally. Tail regrowth also restores pigmentation and sensory structures. This is one of the most studied examples of vertebrate regeneration and continues to fascinate scientists.

Why This Matters

Shows remarkable resilience and functional restoration capabilities.

It provides models for studying integrated tissue regeneration and developmental biology.

Did You Know?

They can regrow a full tail multiple times in a lifetime with no permanent impairment.

Source

Journal of Morphology (wiley.com)
