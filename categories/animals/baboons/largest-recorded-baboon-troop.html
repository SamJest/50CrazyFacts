Largest Recorded Baboon Troop

Some baboon troops have been recorded with over 300 individuals, an extreme social aggregation.

In rare regions of Kenya and Tanzania, troops exceeding 300 members have been observed. These mega-troops require complex social organization to maintain hierarchy, prevent conflict, and coordinate movement. Leadership roles are distributed among multiple high-ranking males and elder females. Foraging logistics become critical, as food resources must be efficiently exploited. Such large groups increase protection against predators but also elevate disease transmission risk. Researchers study these extremes to understand limits of social cohesion and resource competition. Troop size fluctuates seasonally depending on resource availability. Mega-troops illustrate the upper bounds of baboon social capacity. Extreme troop size is rare but provides a natural laboratory for studying social dynamics.

Why This Matters

Significance: Shows the capacity of baboons for complex, large-scale social organization.

Large troop behavior offers insight into human social evolution parallels.

Did You Know?

Some mega-troops exhibit over 15 distinct male-male coalitions simultaneously.
