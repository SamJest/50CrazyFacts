Bandicoots Can Hold Their Breath Underwater

They’re semi-aquatic escape artists.

💥 Impact

Underwater breath-holding expands habitat range and provides predator evasion strategies.

It also demonstrates evolutionary versatility in small terrestrial mammals.

🤯 Did You Know?

Bandicoots can hold their breath and swim underwater to escape predators or forage.

Bandicoots, though primarily terrestrial, are capable swimmers and can hold their breath underwater for up to a minute. They use this ability to cross streams, escape predators, or forage near water bodies. Observations show that when threatened, they dive into water, propelling themselves with strong hind legs while keeping tiny nostrils above or just beneath the surface. Fossil analysis indicates that ancestral bandicoots likely inhabited swampy environments, so this skill is deeply rooted in evolutionary history. Holding breath underwater also allows them to evade predators like snakes, foxes, and raptors who cannot pursue in aquatic conditions. Watching a bandicoot vanish into reeds and resurface moments later reveals a mini James Bond in marsupial form. This behavior blurs the line between terrestrial and semi-aquatic niches, giving them ecological flexibility that larger marsupials often lack. It’s a reminder that small mammals can possess surprising versatility in movement and survival tactics.
