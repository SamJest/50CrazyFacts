Bandicoots Can Echolocate Like Bats (Sort Of)

Tiny mammals with sonic secrets.

💥 Impact

Echo-based navigation helps bandicoots move efficiently and safely in dense nocturnal habitats.

It also illustrates early-stage sensory innovation preceding complex echolocation systems.

🤯 Did You Know?

Bandicoots use high-frequency calls to navigate dense vegetation at night.

Some research suggests that bandicoots emit high-frequency calls and listen for returning echoes to navigate dense undergrowth at night. While not true echolocation like bats, this primitive system aids in obstacle detection and prey localization. Field experiments with ultrasonic recorders detected bandicoot calls correlating with complex terrain features, implying acoustic spatial awareness. Evolutionarily, this adaptation allows nocturnal marsupials to exploit environments where vision is limited. Aboriginal trackers reportedly noted the precise movement of bandicoots in near-total darkness, hinting at their auditory navigation skills. The combination of acute hearing and these calls turns the forest floor into a sonar landscape, with tiny creatures mapping space in real time. Observing them bounce between shrubs without collision, one might wonder if they have secret sonar maps etched in their minds. It’s an underappreciated survival toolkit in a world dominated by darkness after sundown.
