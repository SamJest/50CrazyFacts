Queen Victoria’s Name Was Almost Alexandrina

Her birth name wasn’t Victoria at all. History nearly chose a very different queenly title.

Queen Victoria was born Alexandrina Victoria, named after her godfather Tsar Alexander I of Russia. As a child, she was called “Drina” by family members. When she ascended the throne in 1837, she deliberately chose Victoria as her regnal name. The decision helped distance her reign from foreign associations and sounded more distinctly British. At the time, choosing a regnal name was a strategic branding move. The name “Victoria” soon became synonymous with an entire era of British dominance. Her choice permanently shaped how history remembers the 19th century.

Why This Matters

The name decision shows how monarchy uses symbolism to project power and identity.

It also proves how one personal choice can redefine an entire historical period.

Did You Know?

If she had chosen Alexandrina, we might call it the Alexandrian Era instead.
