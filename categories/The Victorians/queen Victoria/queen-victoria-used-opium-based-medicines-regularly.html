Queen Victoria Used Opium-Based Medicines Regularly

Her doctors prescribed drugs that would shock modern readers.

Like many Victorians, Queen Victoria was prescribed opium-based treatments for pain, anxiety, and sleep. Laudanum, a common opiate medicine, was widely accepted and legal. Doctors used it for childbirth discomfort and emotional distress. Victoria documented its effects carefully in her diaries. While effective short-term, it carried risks now well understood. The practice reflects the era’s limited medical knowledge. Her experience mirrors broader Victorian medical habits.

Why This Matters

This fact highlights how medical science evolves over time.

It also shows how even royalty was subject to experimental treatments.

Did You Know?

Laudanum was sold over the counter in Victorian Britain.
