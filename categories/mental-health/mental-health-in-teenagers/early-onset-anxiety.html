Early Onset Anxiety

Anxiety disorders often emerge in adolescence.

💥 Impact

Adolescent anxiety is both common and biologically influenced.

Early identification and treatment improve lifelong mental health outcomes.

🤯 Did You Know?

Anxiety disorders often first appear during teenage years.

Epidemiological studies indicate that up to 31% of teens experience significant anxiety symptoms before age 18, often triggered by social, academic, or familial stress. Historically, anxious teens were labeled as shy or over-sensitive, delaying intervention. Neurodevelopmental factors, including hyperactive amygdala responses and delayed prefrontal control, contribute to heightened vigilance and fear responses. Chronic anxiety interferes with sleep, academic performance, and social development. Early detection through school programs, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and supportive parenting improves long-term outcomes. Understanding that anxiety often emerges during this developmental window reframes early intervention as critical. This also helps reduce stigma by explaining that anxiety is a biologically and socially mediated disorder, not a personal flaw.

Source

Journal of Anxiety Disorders
