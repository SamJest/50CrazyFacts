PTSD and Shipwreck Survivors

Maritime disasters left minds as shattered as ships.

💥 Impact

Maritime PTSD illustrates that trauma is not only modern or military-specific.

It underscores that extreme environmental stress can have long-term psychological consequences.

🤯 Did You Know?

Survivors of shipwrecks in the 1700s displayed PTSD-like symptoms long before the term existed.

Historical accounts of sailors and passengers surviving shipwrecks describe post-trauma phenomena identical to modern PTSD: insomnia, hypervigilance, panic attacks, and flashbacks. In the 18th century, survivors of the wreck of the HMS Victory exhibited severe anxiety and ritualized behaviors long after rescue. The combination of helplessness, sensory overload, and existential threat activated the same fight-or-flight circuitry that modern neuroscience links to PTSD. Diaries reveal obsessive recounting of events, intrusive memories, and emotional numbing. Society often dismissed these symptoms as hysteria or weakness, leaving survivors socially isolated. The horror wasn’t only in the waves—it persisted in memory, reshaping personalities and relationships. Studying maritime trauma offers insight into how acute, uncontrollable events imprint indelibly on the human psyche.
