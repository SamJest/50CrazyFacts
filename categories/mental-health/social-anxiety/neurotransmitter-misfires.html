Neurotransmitter Misfires

Serotonin, GABA, and dopamine tangled.

💥 Impact

Neurochemical imbalances intensify social threat perception.

It underscores why therapy often combines biological and psychological approaches.

🤯 Did You Know?

Social anxiety arises from dysregulated neurotransmitter systems affecting threat perception.

Chemical imbalances aren’t inherently “wrong”—they reflect a hyper-reactive social evaluation system. Low GABA activity reduces inhibitory control, serotonin dysregulation amplifies sensitivity to rejection, and dopamine irregularities skew reward prediction, making social interactions feel unpredictable. Evolutionarily, this cocktail tuned the brain to detect allies, rivals, and subtle threats. Modern labs confirm that pharmacological interventions—SSRIs, GABA modulators—temporarily stabilize circuits, offering relief while therapies train cognitive regulation. It’s like upgrading a vintage alarm system to avoid false fire alerts. The brain chemistry behind social anxiety is ancient circuitry meeting contemporary social complexity, creating profound internal tension.
