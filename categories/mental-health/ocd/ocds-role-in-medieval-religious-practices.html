OCD’s Role in Medieval Religious Practices

Religious rituals sometimes masked OCD.

💥 Impact

Historical religious obsessions show how OCD symptoms can be misunderstood across cultures.

Modern clinicians learn from past misinterpretations to improve diagnosis and treatment.

🤯 Did You Know?

Medieval religious rituals often reflected OCD-like patterns, particularly scrupulosity.

In medieval monasteries, some monks repeatedly confessed sins or performed endless prayers in patterns resembling modern OCD. These behaviors were often interpreted as extreme piety rather than pathology, but diaries and letters reveal that anxiety and intrusive thoughts were central drivers. One monk documented performing the same prayer sequence 20 times because he feared divine punishment for mistakes. Modern psychiatry recognizes such behaviors as “scrupulosity,” an OCD subtype. Historical framing illustrates how cultural context shapes whether obsessive behaviors are labeled moral failure or mental disorder. It also suggests that religious environments may exacerbate compulsive rituals, providing both structure and stress triggers simultaneously. Understanding these roots informs contemporary treatment, including cognitive-behavioral therapy tailored for scrupulosity.
