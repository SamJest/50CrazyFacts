OCD and World War Trauma

Military stress accelerated compulsive behaviors.

💥 Impact

Studying wartime OCD cases shows that trauma can reshape compulsive behaviors.

It informs PTSD and OCD comorbidity research and intervention strategies.

🤯 Did You Know?

Extreme stress during wars historically exacerbated OCD-like rituals in soldiers.

Accounts from World War I and II describe soldiers developing ritualistic behaviors to manage anxiety and trauma. Some compulsively checked weapons, washed hands to avoid contamination, or repeated prayers, mirroring OCD symptomatology. While initially dismissed as “nervousness” or morale issues, modern analysis suggests that extreme stress and hypervigilance can trigger or worsen OCD. Military hospitals recorded cases where repetitive rituals became debilitating, preventing soldiers from performing duties. These historical cases reveal how environmental pressures interact with neurological vulnerability, highlighting OCD’s adaptive origins as a coping mechanism. Neuroscience today links heightened threat detection circuits to both anxiety and compulsive repetition, bridging historical observation and modern biology.

Source

Journal of Anxiety Disorders
