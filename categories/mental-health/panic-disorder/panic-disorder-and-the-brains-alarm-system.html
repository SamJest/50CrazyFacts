Panic Disorder and the Brain’s Alarm System

Why does your body feel attacked during a panic episode?

💥 Impact

Explaining the neurobiology illuminates why panic episodes are uncontrollable and physically intense.

Reveals treatment strategies that target the brain-body feedback loop for lasting relief.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know panic disorder hijacks your brain’s survival alarm, triggering terror in safe situations?

Panic disorder hijacks the body’s “alarm system,” primarily the amygdala and locus coeruleus. When triggered, these neural circuits flood the body with adrenaline, causing heart palpitations, sweating, and shortness of breath. Early researchers in the 20th century observed that sufferers often had heightened baseline sensitivity to physiological arousal. Evolutionary psychologists argue this hypervigilance stems from survival mechanisms that overreacted in safe modern environments. The paradox is remarkable: a system designed to save life now generates terror in benign situations. Cognitive-behavioral studies show sufferers misinterpret bodily signals, amplifying panic. Neuroimaging confirms abnormal connectivity between fear-processing and regulatory regions. This makes panic disorder both a physiological and perceptual phenomenon. Treatment strategies leverage this understanding, combining exposure therapy with pharmacological interventions targeting these circuits. Understanding the alarm system clarifies why panic feels “real” even without real threat.
