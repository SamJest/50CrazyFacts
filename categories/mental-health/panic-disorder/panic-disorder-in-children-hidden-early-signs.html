Panic Disorder in Children: Hidden Early Signs

Can kids panic without words?

💥 Impact

Highlights early and subtle manifestations of panic disorder in children.

Supports pediatric screening and early intervention programs.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know children can experience panic attacks without being able to explain them?

While panic disorder is often studied in adults, children can exhibit subtle early signs, including stomachaches, refusal to go to school, or sudden clinginess. Historical pediatric case studies reveal that misdiagnosis as “temper tantrums” delayed treatment for years. Neurologically, the developing amygdala and prefrontal cortex may amplify physiological fear responses, making panic attacks more intense yet harder to verbalize. Interestingly, some children may express panic through aggression or withdrawal rather than verbal complaints. Early intervention, including play therapy and parent-guided CBT, improves outcomes significantly. Pediatric awareness reduces stigma and prevents chronic anxiety trajectories into adulthood. Recognizing atypical presentations helps educators and clinicians differentiate panic from behavioral issues. Families empowered with knowledge can respond proactively rather than reactively. Childhood panic is thus not rare—it’s often invisible without careful observation.

Source

Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry
