World’s Oldest ADHD Diagnosis

Historical records suggest ADHD-like traits were noted in the 1700s.

💥 Impact

Crichton’s documentation reframes ADHD as a longstanding human condition. It proves attention differences are not modern inventions.

Early historical records validate ADHD as a persistent neurological variation, not a cultural trend.

🤯 Did You Know?

ADHD-like symptoms were documented as early as 1798 by Sir Alexander Crichton.

In 1798, Sir Alexander Crichton, a Scottish physician, documented children with “incapacity of attending with necessary constancy.” This predates modern psychiatry by decades, describing hyperactivity, impulsivity, and distractibility in precise detail. Crichton observed these traits persisted into adulthood, contradicting the myth that hyperactive children simply “outgrow” it. The description was meticulous: attention shifts, sudden obsessions, and social restlessness. He distinguished these behaviors from moral failing or laziness—a revolutionary stance for the era. Observing ADHD without classrooms, smartphones, or standardized tests highlights the universality of these traits. It’s remarkable that such nuanced observations were made with pen, ink, and keen perception alone. Crichton’s work is a testament to early clinical insight into neurodevelopmental variation.
