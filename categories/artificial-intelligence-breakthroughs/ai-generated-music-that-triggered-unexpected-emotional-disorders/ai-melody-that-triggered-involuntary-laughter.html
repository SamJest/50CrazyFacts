At MIT, researchers created 'GiggleTone,' an AI composition combining irregular rhythms, unexpected harmonic shifts, and subtle pitch modulations. Participants reported sudden bouts of laughter, sometimes lasting several minutes, without any jokes or visual cues. fMRI scans revealed heightened activity in the brain’s reward and mirror neuron systems, suggesting an automatic social-emotional response. The AI had unintentionally blended patterns statistically linked to contagious laughter in prior auditory datasets. Some listeners described laughing in sync with imagined companions, even when alone. GiggleTone became the first AI-generated music documented to reliably induce involuntary laughter. Effects were temporary but intense, leaving participants both amused and puzzled. Researchers noted the potential for AI to modulate emotional contagion and social behavior. The track demonstrated AI’s capacity to trigger strong emotional responses through purely auditory stimuli.
