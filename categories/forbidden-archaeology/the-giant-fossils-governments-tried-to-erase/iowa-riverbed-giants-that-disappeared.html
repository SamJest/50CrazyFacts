During the 1880s, crews dredging Iowa’s rivers reported skeletal remains between 10 and 12 feet tall. The bones had massive femurs, broad rib cages, and elongated skulls. Copper and stone tools were buried alongside, suggesting ceremonial significance. Workers recorded sketches and letters documenting the finds, but federal authorities allegedly confiscated all evidence. Eyewitnesses noted footprints several feet long impressed in riverbanks. Local folklore mentions 'giant river people,' hinting at a cultural memory preserved through oral tradition. Later construction and river modifications obliterated the original sites. Scholars debate whether the remains were human or misidentified megafauna, but eyewitness consistency is notable.
