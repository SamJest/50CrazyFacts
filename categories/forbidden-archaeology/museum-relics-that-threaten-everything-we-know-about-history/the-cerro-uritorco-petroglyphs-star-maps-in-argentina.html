Certain carvings align with the Orion constellation as it appeared over 3,000 years ago, verified using modern astronomy software.

Located in , a series of petroglyphs depict intricate spirals, lines, and patterns aligned with solstices and equinoxes. Estimated to be over 3,000 years old, these carvings correspond to the rising and setting positions of prominent stars and planets. Anthropologists suggest the petroglyphs served as both ritualistic and observational tools, linking agricultural cycles to celestial events. Some symbols resemble those found in Sumerian and Near Eastern star charts, sparking debate about possible trans-oceanic knowledge transfer. The carvings demonstrate sophisticated understanding of astronomy, measurement, and timekeeping. Their precision challenges assumptions about pre-Columbian scientific knowledge. Modern software simulations confirm the alignments, suggesting intentional design rather than coincidence. The site continues to intrigue archaeologists, astronomers, and historians alike. It highlights how ancient cultures may have encoded knowledge into art and landscape.
