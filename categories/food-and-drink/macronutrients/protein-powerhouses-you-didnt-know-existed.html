Protein Powerhouses You Didn't Know Existed

Think you know protein? Some edible insects pack more protein per gram than beef. Curious how?

💥 Impact

Finding alternative protein sources challenges our culinary assumptions and expands our understanding of nutrition. The protein content of insects highlights the surprising versatility of macronutrients beyond conventional sources.

It reshapes global protein strategies, especially amid climate change. Encouraging insect protein could make diets more sustainable while maintaining muscle and energy intake.

🤯 Did You Know?

Crickets contain more protein per gram than beef and are rich in fiber and micronutrients, yet many cultures ignore them.

Insects like crickets and mealworms are little protein bombs, often surpassing traditional meats in essential amino acids. While Western diets scoff at edible insects, cultures in Africa, Asia, and Latin America have relied on them for centuries. The nutritional science shows that gram for gram, crickets can provide up to 65% protein, compared to beef at roughly 26%. Beyond protein, they also deliver fiber and micronutrients often missing in modern diets. Imagine protein bars made from ground crickets—less water, less methane, same muscle-building potential. Scientists even argue that embracing insect protein could reduce global greenhouse gas emissions dramatically. Yet, despite all this, the "ick" factor stalls mass adoption in many countries. The real kicker? You might have eaten protein from insects already, unknowingly, in processed foods.
