The Sourdough Pandemic Phenomenon

Yeast turned into a lockdown hobby.

💥 Impact

Behavioral responses to crisis can trigger culinary trends, showing how emotion, routine, and sharing catalyze adoption. Social media amplified hobbyist culture globally.

Sourdough baking in 2020 illustrates how traditional food methods can surge in popularity during societal upheaval, blending craft, comfort, and community.

🤯 Did You Know?

Sourdough baking became a global pandemic trend in 2020, uniting history, comfort, and social sharing.

During the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, sourdough bread baking exploded worldwide. Flour and yeast shortages occurred as home bakers experimented with wild fermentation. Social media became a classroom, with people sharing starter tips, shaping techniques, and creative recipes. Sourdough’s appeal lay in both culinary satisfaction and psychological comfort during isolation. Ancient fermentation techniques met modern DIY culture, creating a trend with roots in history but unprecedented contemporary adoption. Bakers tracked hydration ratios like scientists, documenting crust coloration and crumb density. The trend revealed that food crazes can emerge spontaneously during societal disruption. People found community through shared culinary endeavors. Starter jars became prized possessions and even gifts. The pandemic turned a traditional craft into an instant, global obsession.
