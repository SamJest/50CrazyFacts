Vegan Fermented Beverages Existed Long Before Kombucha

Could monks have been drinking plant-based “probiotics” centuries ago?

💥 Impact

Plant-based fermented drinks highlight how nutrition, preservation, and taste co-evolved in historical diets.

They demonstrate that functional vegan beverages predate contemporary health trends.

🤯 Did You Know?

Vegan fermented beverages were an essential part of medieval nutrition and culture.

In medieval Europe and Asia, plant-based fermented drinks—like kvass, soy-based drinks, and lightly fermented fruit beverages—were common. Monks documented preparation methods emphasizing hygiene, fermentation time, and ingredient balance to maximize flavor and nutrition. These beverages provided hydration, energy, and digestive benefits during fasting periods, making them indispensable. Some recipes included herbs or spices, adding complexity and medicinal value. Fermentation allowed storage without refrigeration, critical for monastic and rural life. Historical evidence shows careful experimentation to control acidity, carbonation, and shelf-life. Such drinks were often celebrated in community feasts, indicating that plant-based probiotics were both functional and social. Modern vegan probiotic trends echo these centuries-old practices. This shows that fermentation has always been an intersection of science, diet, and culture.
