Vitamin K: The Coagulation and Bone Regulator

Think vitamin K is only about clotting? Think again.

💥 Impact

Vitamin K regulates clotting, bone health, and cardiovascular integrity.

Deficiency historically caused severe bleeding disorders.

🤯 Did You Know?

Vitamin K literally directs calcium where it’s needed.

Vitamin K, encompassing K1 (phylloquinone) and K2 (menaquinone), is essential for blood coagulation, bone mineralization, and cardiovascular health. Historically, deficiency led to uncontrolled bleeding in infants and malnourished adults. Leafy greens, fermented foods, and certain animal products supply vitamin K. It activates proteins that bind calcium, crucial for both clot formation and skeletal integrity. Emerging research links vitamin K2 to arterial health, showing how it redirects calcium from arteries into bones. Deficiency may be subtle, yet impactful, affecting bone density and cardiovascular function. Appreciating vitamin K highlights the intricate biochemical choreography that balances bleeding and skeletal strength. Its dual role exemplifies how micronutrients can multitask invisibly yet vitally.
