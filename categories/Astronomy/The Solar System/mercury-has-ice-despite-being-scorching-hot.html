Mercury Has Ice Despite Being Scorching Hot

Water exists where you’d least expect.

Mercury is closest to the Sun, with surface temperatures soaring over 430°C (800°F). Yet, its permanently shadowed craters near the poles contain frozen water ice. These regions never see sunlight, allowing ice to survive for billions of years. Detecting ice on Mercury informs theories about water distribution in the inner solar system.

Why This Matters

It matters because water is key for understanding planetary evolution and potential life.

It also raises questions about how volatile compounds can survive extreme environments.

Did You Know?

Mercury has ice deposits in craters despite temperatures over 800°F.
