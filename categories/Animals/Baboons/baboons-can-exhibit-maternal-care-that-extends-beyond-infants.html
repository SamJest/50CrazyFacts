Baboons Can Exhibit Maternal Care That Extends Beyond Infants

Mothers aren’t the only caregivers.

Female baboons sometimes care for or protect the offspring of relatives or close allies. This “alloparental care” includes grooming, babysitting, and defending against threats. Such behavior strengthens social bonds, improves troop cohesion, and ensures the survival of genetically or socially related young. Young baboons learn social skills through interactions with multiple caretakers. Alloparenting demonstrates cooperative strategies beyond immediate family.

Why This Matters

Highlights cooperative care as an evolutionary strategy.

It emphasizes social complexity and the benefits of shared parenting.

Did You Know?

They may babysit or groom the offspring of allies while their mothers forage.

Source

Madagascar Primate Research Program (mprp.mg)
