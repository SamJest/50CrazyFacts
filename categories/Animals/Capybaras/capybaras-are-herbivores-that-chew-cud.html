Capybaras Are Herbivores That Chew Cud

They’re like tiny cows of the rainforest.

Capybaras primarily feed on grasses, aquatic plants, and fruits. They are hindgut fermenters, meaning they digest tough cellulose in their intestines, and they practice coprophagy—eating their own feces—to extract more nutrients. Chewing cud allows them to maximize energy from low-nutrient vegetation. Their teeth grow continuously to handle the constant wear of fibrous plants. Seasonal changes in diet are common, with more aquatic vegetation eaten during floods. This diet and digestive strategy make them highly efficient herbivores.

Why This Matters

Highlights evolutionary adaptation to low-quality vegetation.

It demonstrates the role of diet in shaping physiology and behavior.

Did You Know?

They can eat large amounts of grass daily—up to 8 kilograms (17 pounds).
