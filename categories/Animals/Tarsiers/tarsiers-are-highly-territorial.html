Tarsiers Are Highly Territorial

Territory zeal.

Male tarsiers fiercely defend territories using vocalizations, scent marking, and occasional physical displays. Territory size depends on food availability and population density. Defense reduces competition and secures reproductive opportunities. Territorial behavior minimizes encounters with rival males, lowering injury risk. Females may overlap with male territories but generally respect boundaries. Territoriality is critical for survival and reproductive success in fragmented forests. It also structures social interactions and space use. Knowledge of territorial behavior informs conservation planning.

Why This Matters

Territoriality ensures resource control and mating success.

It reduces conflicts and supports population stability.

Did You Know?

Male tarsiers are highly territorial and defend areas with calls and scent marking.
