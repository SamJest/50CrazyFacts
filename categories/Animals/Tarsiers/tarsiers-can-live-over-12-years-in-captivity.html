Tarsiers Can Live Over 12 Years in Captivity

Longevity under care.

Tarsiers have a lifespan of up to 12–20 years in captivity, though in the wild it’s typically shorter. Proper diet, habitat, and minimal stress contribute to extended life. Longevity allows for longer reproductive periods in managed care. Studying captive individuals informs conservation strategies and behavior research. Extended lifespans highlight resilience when environmental pressures are mitigated. Tarsiers’ survival in captivity contrasts sharply with vulnerability in fragmented forests. Longevity also supports educational and awareness programs. Lifespan data guide long-term species management.

Why This Matters

Extended life aids research and breeding programs.

It provides insight into species needs and conservation potential.
