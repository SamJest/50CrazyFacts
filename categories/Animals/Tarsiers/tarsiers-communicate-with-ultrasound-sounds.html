Tarsiers Communicate With Ultrasound Sounds

Hidden conversations.

Tarsiers emit ultrasonic calls above human hearing range. These sounds help them communicate territory, mating readiness, and family signals without alerting predators. Ultrasound allows discreet communication in dense rainforests. They can adjust frequency and intensity to reach specific individuals. Vocalization supplements scent-marking and visual cues, forming a sophisticated communication network. It is essential for social cohesion in solitary and small-group living. Researchers study these calls to understand primate evolution and communication complexity.

Why This Matters

Ultrasonic communication protects them from predators.

It enables discreet social interaction and reproductive signaling.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers can communicate using ultrasonic sounds that are inaudible to humans.
