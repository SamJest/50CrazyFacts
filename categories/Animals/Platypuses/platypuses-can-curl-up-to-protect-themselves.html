Platypuses Can Curl Up to Protect Themselves

Safe burrowing.

When threatened, platypuses can curl their bodies and tuck limbs and tail close to hide. Their dense, waterproof fur also provides cushioning and insulation. In burrows, this posture minimizes exposure to predators. Males may also display spurs if cornered. Juveniles adopt curling as a defense early on. It is an instinctive survival behavior. This strategy works alongside camouflage and nocturnal activity.

Why This Matters

Curling enhances defense in vulnerable situations.

It increases survival odds against predators or human disturbance.

Did You Know?

Platypuses curl into compact positions for protection in burrows.
