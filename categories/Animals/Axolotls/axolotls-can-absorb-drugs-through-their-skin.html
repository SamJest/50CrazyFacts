Axolotls Can Absorb Drugs Through Their Skin

The axolotl’s skin isn’t just for show—it’s a pharmaceutical gateway.

Axolotls can take up chemicals, including medications, directly through their skin, bypassing digestion. This property makes them valuable for testing topical and systemic drugs in research. Their permeable skin, combined with an aquatic lifestyle, allows molecules in water to enter their bloodstream efficiently. Scientists have used this trait to study antibiotics, anti-inflammatories, and growth factors without injections. The process is influenced by skin thickness, mucus layer, and temperature. It provides a painless and non-invasive method for research compared to other animal models. The axolotl’s skin absorption also explains why water quality is crucial in captivity.

Why This Matters

This shows how amphibians’ physiology can inform pharmacology and environmental medicine.

It also underscores the responsibility of keeping habitats and tanks free from harmful substances.

Did You Know?

Medications placed in water can reach their bloodstream in just minutes.

Source

Frontiers in Physiology (frontiersin.org)
