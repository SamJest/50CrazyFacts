Axolotls Can Eat Without Swallowing—They Suck Up Food Like Vacuum Cleaners!

Forget chewing—axolotls have a feeding method that seems like magic. They use a suction mechanism to pull in prey almost instantly.

Axolotls open their mouths rapidly and expand their throat cavity to create a vacuum, pulling in water and food simultaneously. This allows them to swallow prey as big as small fish without chewing. Tiny teeth line their mouths but mainly serve to hold food rather than shred it. This suction feeding is highly efficient in murky lakes, where visibility is low and speed is essential. Even insects and tiny worms can be eaten this way in a split second. Scientists marvel at how precisely they can control the pressure to avoid injuring themselves. This technique has inspired robotic engineers designing underwater suction devices. The method is so effective that axolotls rarely miss a meal in their natural habitat.

Why This Matters

Understanding their suction feeding helps ecologists study aquatic food chains and predator-prey interactions.

It also demonstrates how evolution can invent solutions that seem fantastical to humans.

Did You Know?

Axolotls can capture prey in less than a hundredth of a second, faster than the blink of an eye.
