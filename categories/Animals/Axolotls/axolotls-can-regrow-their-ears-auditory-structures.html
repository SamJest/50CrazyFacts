Axolotls Can Regrow Their Ears (Auditory Structures)

Even sensory organs like ears can be fully restored in axolotls.

If an axolotl’s external ear structure or inner auditory tissue is damaged, regeneration restores it completely. This involves stem-cell-like cells forming new cartilage, skin, and sensory hair cells. Hearing function is fully recovered, allowing them to detect prey and predators normally. This regeneration is coordinated with nervous system reconnections for proper sensory input. No other vertebrate demonstrates such comprehensive auditory regeneration. Studying this can inform research into hearing loss and tissue engineering. It’s a remarkable example of how axolotls rebuild complex functional structures.

Why This Matters

Highlights the breadth of their regenerative abilities across organ systems.

It provides a unique biological model for sensory organ repair research.

Did You Know?

Auditory structures can regrow with restored functionality in a few weeks.

Source

Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology (frontiersin.org)
