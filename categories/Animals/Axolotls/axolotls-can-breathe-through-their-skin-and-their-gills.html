Axolotls Can Breathe Through Their Skin—And Their Gills!

Why choose one method of breathing when you can have three? Axolotls are equipped for maximum oxygen intake.

Axolotls have feathery external gills for underwater respiration, but they can also absorb oxygen through their skin and lungs. This tribranch system allows them to survive in low-oxygen waters where other animals would struggle. Their skin must remain moist to facilitate gas exchange. In captivity, oxygenation of water is critical because their skin respiration is limited in stagnant water. This flexibility allows them to adjust breathing according to environmental conditions. They can even compensate if one respiratory organ is damaged, showing remarkable resilience. Their multi-method breathing is one reason axolotls thrive in both lakes and laboratory tanks.

Why This Matters

Multiple breathing methods improve survival in unpredictable aquatic environments.

It highlights the evolutionary ingenuity of amphibians and why axolotls are model organisms for studying respiratory adaptations.

Did You Know?

They can survive entirely without lungs for extended periods if their gills and skin are healthy.
