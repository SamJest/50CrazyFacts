Axolotls Can Live for Over 15 Years in Captivity

These little aquatic creatures are surprisingly long-lived compared to other amphibians.

While many salamanders have shorter lifespans, axolotls can live 15–20 years in well-maintained aquariums. Lifespan depends on diet, water quality, and stress levels. Their slow metabolism, neoteny, and aquatic lifestyle contribute to longevity. Captive axolotls benefit from controlled temperatures and abundant food, while wild populations often live shorter lives due to predation and habitat loss. Longevity allows for extended observation in research studies, especially for regeneration experiments. It also means that axolotl owners must commit to long-term care. Extended life provides unique opportunities to study aging, growth, and regeneration across many years.

Why This Matters

Long lifespans make them excellent subjects for longitudinal biological studies.

It also emphasizes responsibility for pet owners and the importance of habitat preservation.

Did You Know?

Some captive axolotls have been documented living up to 25 years.
