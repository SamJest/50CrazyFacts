Axolotls Can Sense Magnetic Fields to Navigate Their Environment

These aquatic creatures might have a hidden GPS system—they can detect Earth’s magnetic field.

Research suggests axolotls possess magnetoreception, allowing them to orient themselves and navigate their habitat. Tiny iron-rich particles in their body, possibly in combination with specialized neurons, detect magnetic fields. This helps them find feeding grounds, mates, and safe hiding spots. Though the mechanism isn’t fully understood, studies indicate orientation patterns align with geomagnetic cues. This trait is rare among amphibians, making axolotls especially interesting for sensory biology. Magnetoreception adds another layer to their survival toolkit, alongside lateral line sensing and regeneration. It’s a biological feature that seems almost sci-fi.

Why This Matters

Understanding magnetoreception helps reveal how animals interact with the planet’s magnetic environment.

It may inspire navigational technology or shed light on migration in other species.

Did You Know?

They may realign their bodies in tanks to match magnetic north.
