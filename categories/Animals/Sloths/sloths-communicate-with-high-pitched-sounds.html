Sloths Communicate With High-Pitched Sounds

Secret talk.

Although mostly silent, sloths emit high-pitched calls, especially during mating or distress. The sounds can travel through dense rainforest foliage without revealing location to predators. Babies produce distinct squeaks to signal mothers. Adults occasionally use low-frequency hisses or screeches during encounters. Communication is rare but highly functional, balancing safety and social needs. This vocalization strategy allows sloths to remain hidden yet connected. Calls are essential for mating, offspring care, and territoriality.

Why This Matters

High-pitched sounds facilitate quiet communication.

It ensures social coordination without risking predation.

Did You Know?

Baby sloths use unique squeaks to call for their mothers in dense forests.
