Sloths Have Algae Growing on Their Fur

A tiny green ecosystem.

Sloth fur hosts a mini-ecosystem of algae, fungi, and insects. The algae provide camouflage, blending the sloth with the rainforest canopy. Sloths’ slow movements and limited grooming allow the algae to thrive. Some algae species are unique to sloth fur. This ecosystem can also provide nutrients through incidental ingestion. Moths live in the fur and lay eggs in sloth droppings, continuing a symbiotic cycle. The combination of green fur and slow movement creates near invisibility in the trees.

Why This Matters

Algae growth is an adaptive camouflage strategy.

It also supports biodiversity in the rainforest canopy.

Did You Know?

Sloth fur can host algae, creating a living camouflage that hides them from predators.
