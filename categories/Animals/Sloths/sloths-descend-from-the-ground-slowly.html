Sloths Descend From the Ground Slowly

Arboreal specialists.

Sloths spend most of their lives in trees, descending to the ground only to defecate, which is risky. They descend slowly to conserve energy and avoid predator detection. They dig shallow holes to bury their feces, which supports tree nutrient cycling. The slow descent can take up to 30 minutes, exposing them briefly to jaguars or harpy eagles. This behavior is ritualistic, repeated almost daily. Juveniles practice under maternal supervision to avoid danger. Energy conservation is key during these vulnerable moments.

Why This Matters

Ground visits are a dangerous but necessary behavior.

It contributes to forest ecology through nutrient recycling.

Did You Know?

Sloths descend from trees slowly and carefully to defecate and fertilize the forest floor.
