Aye-Ayes Can Exhibit Seasonal Breeding Patterns

They’re tuned to environmental cycles.

Aye-ayes often breed during certain seasons when food availability is high, ensuring sufficient nutrition for lactating mothers and developing infants. Seasonal breeding synchronizes births to maximize survival chances. Gestation is slow, and infants require prolonged maternal care. Environmental cues like rainfall and fruiting patterns trigger reproductive activity. This strategy aligns reproduction with ecological resources, minimizing infant mortality.

Why This Matters

Shows adaptation of reproduction to environmental conditions.

It highlights the link between ecology and life-history strategies.

Did You Know?

They usually breed when insect larvae and fruits are most abundant in their habitat.

Source

Madagascar Primate Research Program (mprp.mg)
