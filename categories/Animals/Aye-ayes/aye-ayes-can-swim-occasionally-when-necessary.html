Aye-Ayes Can Swim Occasionally When Necessary

Despite being arboreal, they are not strictly tree-bound.

Aye-ayes can swim to cross small streams or reach food sources. They paddle with all four limbs and can remain afloat for short distances. Swimming is not their primary locomotion but demonstrates behavioral flexibility. This ability may help them escape predators or navigate fragmented habitats. Even as primarily arboreal nocturnal creatures, this skill adds to their survival toolkit.

Why This Matters

Shows that survival often requires versatility in behavior.

It highlights adaptation to environmental challenges beyond the species’ main ecological niche.

Did You Know?

They can swim distances up to several meters despite having no webbed feet.
