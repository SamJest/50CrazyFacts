Aye-Ayes Can Live in Trees Over 20 Meters High

They’re fearless vertical navigators of the rainforest canopy.

Aye-ayes are adept climbers capable of reaching the upper canopy where food sources like larvae or fruit may be more abundant. Height access reduces competition with ground-dwelling species and predators. Their long limbs, gripping hands, and balancing tails make vertical movement safe and efficient. Upper-canopy habitats also provide better shelter from certain predators. Arboreal expertise is central to their lifestyle and ecological success.

Why This Matters

Shows adaptation to a three-dimensional habitat for survival and resource exploitation.

It emphasizes the importance of forest canopy structure for arboreal species’ conservation.

Did You Know?

They can climb and forage at heights exceeding 20 meters in mature forests.
