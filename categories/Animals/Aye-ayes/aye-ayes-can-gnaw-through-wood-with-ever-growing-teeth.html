Aye-Ayes Can Gnaw Through Wood With Ever-Growing Teeth

These primates have teeth straight out of a cartoon—continuously growing like rodents.

An aye-aye’s incisors grow throughout its life, allowing it to gnaw through tough tree bark to reach hidden insects. This adaptation is essential because their diet includes larvae protected deep inside wood. The ever-growing nature prevents tooth wear from limiting access to food. Their teeth are sharp, orange-tinted, and self-sharpening through constant use. This trait has evolved independently of rodents and works in harmony with their unique fingers. It’s a perfect example of convergent evolution in dental adaptation.

Why This Matters

Highlights the relationship between diet and morphological evolution.

It also illustrates how specialized tools—like teeth and fingers—co-evolve to solve ecological challenges.

Did You Know?

They can chew through bark up to several millimeters thick to reach prey.
