Aye-Ayes Can Live Up to 25 Years in Captivity

Their longevity exceeds many other nocturnal primates.

Aye-ayes can live 20–25 years in zoos or conservation facilities under proper care. Captive longevity allows extended study of behavior, reproduction, and health. It also provides opportunities for breeding programs to support wild populations. Long life spans underscore the need for sustained habitat protection and generational management in the wild. Studying aging in aye-ayes offers insights into metabolism, disease resistance, and primate longevity.

Why This Matters

Highlights the importance of lifespan in species conservation.

It provides opportunities for long-term ecological and behavioral research.

Did You Know?

They can live more than two decades when properly cared for in captivity.
