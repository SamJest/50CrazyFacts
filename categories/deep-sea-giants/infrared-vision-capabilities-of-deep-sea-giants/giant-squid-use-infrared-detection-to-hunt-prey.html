Giant Squid Use Infrared Detection to Hunt Prey

Giant squids can sense the faint heat emitted by their prey, giving them a night vision advantage in the pitch-black deep sea.

Giant squids have eyes the size of dinner plates, but infrared detection gives them an even greater edge in total darkness.

Giant squids possess specialized photoreceptors that detect infrared wavelengths, allowing them to sense the body heat of fish and other cephalopods. In the dark abyss, where sunlight never penetrates, this adaptation provides a critical hunting advantage. Juvenile marine biologists observing deep-sea footage noted that squids can strike prey with astonishing accuracy even in complete darkness. Infrared sensitivity is thought to complement their massive eyes, which are already the largest in the animal kingdom. Predators using heat detection can conserve energy by reducing unsuccessful hunting attempts. Studies suggest that infrared perception may also help squids avoid larger predators, including sperm whales. This dual-use sensory system represents one of the most sophisticated adaptations among deep-sea giants. It challenges assumptions that vision is useless in extreme darkness. The discovery opens new questions about sensory evolution in extreme environments.
