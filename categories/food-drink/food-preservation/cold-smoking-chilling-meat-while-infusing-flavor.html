Cold Smoking: Chilling Meat While Infusing Flavor

Smoking without cooking extends storage subtly.

Cold smoking exposes meat and fish to smoke at low temperatures (20–30°C), preserving flavor while allowing slow curing. Unlike hot smoking, it doesn’t cook the food, making it ideal for delicate products like salmon and cheese. Cold smoking has been practiced for centuries in Europe and Scandinavia. Combined with salt or sugar curing, it dramatically increases shelf life.

Why This Matters

It preserves delicate foods without altering texture drastically.

It creates gourmet flavors while extending usability.

Did You Know?

Cold-smoked salmon was popular among Viking traders.
