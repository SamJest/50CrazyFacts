Canning Revolution: How Jules Vernes' Favorite Method Saved Food

Food can last years thanks to heat-sealed jars.

Canning was invented in the early 19th century by Nicolas Appert to preserve food for Napoleon's army. Food is sealed in jars or cans and heated to destroy bacteria. This method allowed long-term storage of fruits, meats, and vegetables. Today, canning preserves nutrients and flavors while making foods transportable worldwide. It revolutionized both home cooking and industrial food production.

Why This Matters

Canning ensures safe, long-term food storage for emergencies.

It transformed global trade, enabling foods to be shipped across continents safely.

Did You Know?

Appert tested canning by sealing soup in glass jars and boiling them for hours.
