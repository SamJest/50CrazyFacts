Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART)

NASA’s DART mission hit an asteroid at 15,000 mph to test deflection. The results shocked scientists.

DART deliberately collided with Dimorphos, a moonlet of asteroid Didymos, in 2022. The goal was to measure orbital changes from the impact. Observations showed a measurable shift, proving kinetic impactors can work for planetary defense. Scientists track the aftermath, including debris distribution and trajectory changes. The mission demonstrates practical asteroid deflection technology. It also strengthens international collaboration and public awareness of cosmic hazards.

Why This Matters

DART provides a proof-of-concept for kinetic asteroid deflection, an essential planetary defense milestone.

Results inform future missions, improving calculations, spacecraft design, and global response strategies for potential threats.

Did You Know?

DART’s impact shortened Dimorphos’ orbit by 32 minutes, confirming kinetic deflection potential.
