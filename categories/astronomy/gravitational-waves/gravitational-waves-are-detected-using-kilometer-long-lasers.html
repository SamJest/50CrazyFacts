Gravitational Waves Are Detected Using Kilometer-Long Lasers

The detectors are enormous. Lasers travel kilometers back and forth. All to measure tiny spacetime wiggles.

LIGO uses L-shaped tunnels four kilometers long. Laser beams bounce between mirrors thousands of times. Even a change smaller than an atomic nucleus is detected. Vibrations from trucks, earthquakes, and waves must be filtered out. Extreme precision is required. Earth itself is noisy.

Why This Matters

This shows the engineering extremes needed for gravitational-wave science. Precision pushes technological limits.

The same techniques improve other fields like metrology and optics. Innovation spills beyond astronomy.

Did You Know?

The mirrors are polished smoother than anything else on Earth.
