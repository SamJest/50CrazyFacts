Time-of-Flight Studies of Cosmic Rays

Speed matters when your particle comes from across the galaxy.

💥 Impact

Precise timing reveals the composition and origin of cosmic rays.

It allows the detection of rare particles and the study of galactic propagation.

🤯 Did You Know?

Time-of-flight detectors measure cosmic ray speed and type, key for understanding composition.

By measuring the time it takes cosmic rays to traverse detectors, scientists determine particle velocities, masses, and energy. Time-of-flight systems separate protons from heavier nuclei and electrons, revealing the particle composition of cosmic rays. Instruments like AMS-02 on the ISS and balloon-borne spectrometers rely on precise timing, often to billionths of a second. These measurements inform models of cosmic ray propagation, energy loss, and interaction with magnetic fields. Time-of-flight studies also detect rare antiparticles, shedding light on fundamental physics and dark matter candidates. The technique transforms the transient passage of high-energy particles into detailed scientific data. Each particle becomes a messenger with quantifiable properties, linking its journey across space to laboratory measurements on Earth. This allows astrophysicists to decode the life history of cosmic rays.
