Cosmic Rays Can Create Rare Isotopes

Some isotopes on Earth only exist because cosmic rays smash into atmospheric atoms. These isotopes serve as natural clocks and tracers.

When cosmic rays collide with nitrogen, oxygen, or other atoms in the atmosphere, they can produce isotopes like carbon-14 and beryllium-10. Carbon-14 is crucial for radiocarbon dating, helping archaeologists and geologists date ancient organic matter. Beryllium-10 is used to study ice cores and soil erosion. This cosmic connection was discovered in the 1940s and shows the surprising influence of extraterrestrial particles on Earthly chemistry.

Why This Matters

Understanding this production of isotopes connects astrophysics with geology and archaeology.

It also illustrates the subtle ways cosmic phenomena affect life and science on Earth, linking the universe directly to human knowledge.

Did You Know?

Did you know all living things contain carbon-14 created by cosmic rays striking our atmosphere?
