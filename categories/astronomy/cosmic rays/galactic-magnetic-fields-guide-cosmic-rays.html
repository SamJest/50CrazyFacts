Galactic Magnetic Fields Guide Cosmic Rays

Cosmic rays don’t just travel in straight lines—they are steered by galactic magnetic fields across the Milky Way. This twists their paths in ways that make tracing their origin tricky.

Charged particles like protons and nuclei are deflected by the magnetic fields that thread through galaxies. This bending means a cosmic ray detected on Earth might have traveled thousands of light-years in a convoluted path. Observing these deflections helps astronomers map magnetic fields and understand cosmic ray propagation. The phenomenon was first noted when directional studies of cosmic rays showed no obvious source alignment.

Why This Matters

Mapping cosmic ray trajectories reveals hidden structures in our galaxy, including magnetic “highways” and turbulent zones.

This also affects how we predict cosmic ray arrival on Earth, which is important for satellite safety and understanding the interstellar medium.

Did You Know?

Did you know the Milky Way’s magnetic fields twist cosmic rays into spiral paths, so their true origin is almost impossible to pinpoint?
