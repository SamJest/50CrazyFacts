Cosmic Rays Have Been Studied With Balloons

Before satellites, scientists used high-altitude balloons to measure cosmic rays.

Victor Hess’s pioneering balloon flights in 1912 revealed increased radiation at higher altitudes. Later, in the mid-20th century, sophisticated instruments on balloons measured particle composition and energy. These studies laid the foundation for modern cosmic ray physics and space-based detectors.

Why This Matters

High-altitude balloon experiments demonstrated that careful measurements in extreme environments yield groundbreaking discoveries.

It also inspired decades of research into cosmic rays, particle physics, and space science.

Did You Know?

Did you know Hess carried electroscopes up to 5,300 meters in a balloon to detect cosmic rays?
