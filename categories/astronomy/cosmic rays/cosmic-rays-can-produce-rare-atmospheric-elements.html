Cosmic Rays Can Produce Rare Atmospheric Elements

High-energy cosmic rays generate rare isotopes in the atmosphere that are otherwise scarce on Earth.

When cosmic rays strike atmospheric nitrogen or oxygen, they produce isotopes like tritium and carbon-14. These isotopes are used in dating techniques, studying atmospheric circulation, and monitoring nuclear tests. This discovery linked cosmic phenomena directly to Earthly chemistry.

Why This Matters

Studying these isotopes provides insights into both atmospheric science and cosmic ray physics.

It also contributes to climate research, environmental monitoring, and understanding human impact on atmospheric chemistry.

Did You Know?

Did you know cosmic rays are responsible for creating a measurable amount of tritium in the atmosphere each year?
