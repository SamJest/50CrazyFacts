Cosmic Rays Can Pass Through Mountains

High-energy cosmic rays, especially muons, can travel through hundreds of meters of rock, including mountains, without being stopped.

This property allows scientists to use cosmic rays for imaging large structures. By detecting muons on the opposite side of a mountain, researchers can map its internal density. This technique, called muon tomography, was pioneered in the 1960s and has applications in volcanology and archaeology.

Why This Matters

It allows non-invasive imaging of geological structures and archaeological sites, giving insights without drilling or excavation.

It also inspires new techniques in security scanning, nuclear waste monitoring, and industrial imaging.

Did You Know?

Did you know cosmic ray muons can penetrate 2 kilometers of rock or more?
