The Moon Records Cosmic Ray History

Moon rocks and lunar soil tell a story of cosmic ray bombardment over millions of years. Unlike Earth, the Moon has no atmosphere to block them, making it a cosmic record keeper.

It’s been found that lunar regolith contains isotopes formed by cosmic ray interactions, essentially a history book of space radiation. Apollo astronauts returned samples in the 1960s and 1970s, which were later analyzed for these isotopes. These records allow scientists to study solar activity and cosmic ray intensity over long periods. Cosmic ray exposure ages in moon rocks can even date certain lunar surface features.

Why This Matters

Studying lunar cosmic ray records gives insight into the history of the solar system’s radiation environment. It helps us understand how cosmic rays influence planetary surfaces.

This research also guides space missions, revealing what long-term exposure to cosmic rays might do to astronauts and equipment on the Moon.

Did You Know?

Did you know some lunar rocks have been exposed to cosmic rays for over a billion years?
