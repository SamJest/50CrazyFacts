Cosmic Rays Interact With Atmospheric Nitrogen

Cosmic rays colliding with nitrogen produce secondary particles and isotopes important for science.

These interactions generate carbon-14, which enters the carbon cycle, and neutrons that are measured by detectors. The discovery of this process linked cosmic events to Earth’s chemistry and dating methods. It has been studied since the 1940s using balloons, satellites, and ground observatories.

Why This Matters

Understanding these interactions is crucial for radiocarbon dating and particle physics.

It also highlights the global impact of cosmic phenomena on our planet.

Did You Know?

Did you know every breath you take contains trace carbon-14 formed by cosmic rays?
