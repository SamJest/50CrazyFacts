Cosmic Rays Can Trigger Atmospheric Neutrons

Cosmic rays hitting the atmosphere produce neutrons, which are invisible but important for radiation monitoring.

High-energy cosmic rays interact with nitrogen and oxygen, creating secondary neutrons. These neutrons contribute to background radiation on Earth and are measured by ground-based neutron monitors. This phenomenon has been studied since the 1950s and is important for calibrating radiation instruments.

Why This Matters

Studying secondary neutrons helps us understand radiation levels at high altitudes and in polar regions.

It also contributes to space weather research and protects electronics and humans from radiation hazards.

Did You Know?

Did you know neutron monitors detect cosmic rays indirectly by measuring secondary neutrons they produce in the atmosphere?
