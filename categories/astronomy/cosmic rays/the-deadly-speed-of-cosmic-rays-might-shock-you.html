The Deadly Speed of Cosmic Rays Might Shock You

You might think space is calm and empty, but cosmic rays can move nearly at the speed of light. They carry such intense energy that a single particle can punch through metal. Scientists constantly monitor them to understand their power.

Cosmic rays travel at speeds approaching 300,000 kilometers per second. A single proton hitting a spacecraft can deposit energy equivalent to a small bullet. Early studies in the 1900s measured them using electroscopes, revealing that Earth is under constant bombardment from these particles. High-altitude balloons and satellite detectors now track these invisible intruders.

Why This Matters

Studying their speed reveals the mechanics of particle acceleration in extreme cosmic environments like supernovae. This helps refine our models of the universe.

Faster particles also pose challenges for electronics and human spaceflight, requiring careful engineering and mission planning. The energy they carry can alter chemical reactions in the atmosphere over time.

Did You Know?

Did you know the first cosmic ray detection was accidental, using a simple electroscope left by Victor Hess in a balloon flight in 1912?
