Lunar Orbit Evolution: Moons on the Move

The Moon is slowly drifting away, and it’s measurable.

💥 Impact

Monitoring lunar orbit changes demonstrates celestial mechanics’ predictive power over long timescales.

It offers insight into the interaction between planets and their satellites.

🤯 Did You Know?

The Moon slowly recedes from Earth, lengthening days due to tidal interactions.

Earth’s tidal bulges transfer angular momentum to the Moon, causing it to recede at about 3.8 cm per year. Ancient days were shorter; hundreds of millions of years ago, a day lasted only 22 hours. Celestial mechanics reveals that this process affects Earth's rotation, tides, and even climate over geological timescales. Lunar laser ranging experiments, bouncing lasers off reflectors left by Apollo missions, track the Moon’s motion with millimeter precision. Understanding the Moon’s evolution informs planetary dynamics, tidal dissipation, and long-term stability of orbits. It also provides insight into how satellite systems behave elsewhere in the cosmos. Celestial mechanics here connects the deep past with modern precision experiments. The Moon’s retreat is a slow but relentless example of gravitational bookkeeping in action.
