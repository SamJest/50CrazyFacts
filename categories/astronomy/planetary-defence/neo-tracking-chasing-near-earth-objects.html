NEO Tracking: Chasing Near-Earth Objects

How do we spot potentially deadly space rocks years before impact? With high-tech cosmic radar.

💥 Impact

Tracking NEOs allows humanity to act proactively rather than reactively, giving us time to plan mitigation.

It also highlights that planetary defence relies heavily on observation and data synthesis before any physical intervention.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know that the largest known NEO, 1036 Ganymed, is over 30 kilometers wide and comes close enough to Earth to require monitoring?

Near-Earth Objects (NEOs) include asteroids and comets with orbits that bring them close to Earth. Advanced radar systems, like Goldstone and Arecibo (when operational), allow astronomers to measure their size, shape, spin, and trajectory with remarkable precision. Optical surveys scan the sky nightly, identifying tiny movements among millions of stars, like finding a needle in a cosmic haystack. Historical misses, like asteroid 2004 MN4, illustrate that even a small observational delay can drastically change impact predictions. By combining telescopic imaging with orbital simulations, scientists can predict NEO encounters decades in advance. This data underpins kinetic impact missions, gravity tractor planning, and civil defence strategies. Tracking is so precise that scientists can predict an asteroid’s position to within meters decades ahead. The effort represents a global scientific marathon, with telescopes spread across continents and collaborative international observation campaigns. NEO monitoring blends astronomy, physics, and applied mathematics in a high-stakes celestial chess game.
