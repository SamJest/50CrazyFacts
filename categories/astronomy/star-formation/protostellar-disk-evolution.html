Protostellar Disk Evolution

Where planets may begin their lives.

💥 Impact

Disks regulate star growth and set stage for planet formation.

They highlight the intimate link between stars and planetary systems.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know most stars start life with a rotating disk that may form planets?

Stars are born surrounded by rotating disks of gas and dust, where angular momentum and magnetic forces shape collapse. These disks serve as sites for accretion and jet launching, as well as eventual planet formation. Observations reveal disk structures, gaps, and rings that may indicate planet formation even in early stages. Disk mass, temperature, and chemical composition evolve over time, influencing star growth and potential planetary architectures. Interactions with companion stars or nearby protostars can warp disks or trigger instabilities. Magnetic fields thread disks, regulating accretion and jet alignment. Studying disk evolution provides insight into the earliest stages of planetary system development. Protostellar disks demonstrate that star formation is inseparable from the genesis of planets. Every newborn star carries a blueprint for potential worlds.
