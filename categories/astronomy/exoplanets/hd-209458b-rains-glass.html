HD 209458b “Rains Glass”

Incredible weather exists on hot Jupiters.

💥 Impact

Studying such exoplanet weather helps understand atmospheric physics in extreme environments.

It also inspires models of cloud formation under alien chemistry.

🤯 Did You Know?

On HD 209458b, it quite literally rains molten glass.

HD 209458b, a well-studied hot Jupiter, orbits so close to its star that its atmosphere can contain vaporized silicates. On the night side, these minerals condense into tiny glass droplets, forming exotic rains. Winds exceed 5,000 mph, distributing clouds across the planet. Observations from Hubble and Spitzer detected signatures of this metallic precipitation. The planet is tidally locked, so one side constantly faces the searing star while the other is a permanent nightscape. This leads to extreme temperature gradients, driving the supersonic weather. Imagine storms raining molten rock while you’d fry alive on the day side. Exoplanet meteorology becomes a blend of chemistry, physics, and pure imagination. HD 209458b proves that planets can host phenomena no Earth-based analogy could capture.
