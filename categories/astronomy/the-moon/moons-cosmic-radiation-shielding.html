Moon’s Cosmic Radiation Shielding

The Moon isn’t just rocks; it shields itself in weird ways.

💥 Impact

Lunar soil provides natural radiation shielding for exploration.

It preserves a record of past cosmic activity in the regolith.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know lunar soil can shield astronauts from cosmic radiation and preserve space weather history?

Lunar regolith can absorb cosmic rays and solar particles, providing natural shielding for potential habitats. Experiments show that 2–3 meters of compacted regolith can significantly reduce radiation exposure. This protection is uneven due to surface features like ridges and craters. Understanding regolith shielding is critical for astronaut safety, habitat construction, and long-term missions. Lunar soil also traps volatile compounds affected by radiation, recording space weather history. These layers act as a historical ledger of solar activity over eons. Even a barren landscape can have protective properties, a subtle form of cosmic armor. Lunar radiation studies inform both exploration planning and planetary science.
