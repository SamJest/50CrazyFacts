Moon’s Surface Ages: A Patchwork Quilt

Not all lunar landscapes are equally old. Some are younger than dinosaurs.

💥 Impact

The Moon’s surface preserves billions of years of impact history.

It serves as a cosmic laboratory for planetary dating methods.

🤯 Did You Know?

Did you know the Moon’s surface ranges from over 4 billion to under 100 million years old, creating a geological patchwork?

Lunar craters and maria vary in age, creating a patchwork of geological history. Radiometric dating of returned samples shows that mare basalts are 3–3.5 billion years old, while some highland crust exceeds 4.4 billion years. Small craters continue forming today, meaning the surface is constantly evolving, albeit slowly. Comparing crater density across regions allows scientists to reconstruct impact history and even infer solar system dynamics. Certain “young” craters like Tycho or Copernicus are only hundreds of millions of years old, relatively recent in lunar terms. This patchwork tells a story of bombardment, volcanic resurfacing, and geological quiescence. It’s a record book written in rock and dust, preserved far better than on Earth. Lunar surface ages help calibrate crater-count dating techniques across the Solar System.
