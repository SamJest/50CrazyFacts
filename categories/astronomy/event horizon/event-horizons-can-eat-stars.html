Event Horizons Can “Eat” Stars

Black holes devour with extreme gravity.

If a star ventures too close to a black hole, the event horizon can engulf it entirely in a process called tidal disruption. The star stretches and compresses violently, forming a stream of hot gas before crossing the horizon. This produces luminous flares detectable from Earth. Observing these events helps astrophysicists study stellar structure, black hole mass, and extreme gravity. Tidal disruption events provide some of the brightest signals associated with event horizons.

Why This Matters

Studying these events improves predictions of black hole feeding behaviors.

It also helps identify previously hidden black holes in galaxies.

Did You Know?

Event horizons can literally rip stars apart in cosmic feasts.
