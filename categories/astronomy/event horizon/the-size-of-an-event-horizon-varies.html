The Size of an Event Horizon Varies

Not all horizons are equal.

Event horizon size depends on the mass of the black hole; supermassive black holes can have horizons billions of kilometers wide. Conversely, small stellar black holes have horizons only a few tens of kilometers across. The radius of the event horizon is called the Schwarzschild radius, and it scales linearly with mass. This affects the gravitational pull, the volume of influence, and the nature of accretion processes. The variability in size also changes how surrounding matter behaves and emits radiation. Observing different black holes reveals the diversity of event horizons in the cosmos.

Why This Matters

Understanding horizon size helps measure black hole mass precisely.

It also impacts predictions of gravitational wave signals during mergers.

Did You Know?

Event horizon size can range from tiny stellar scales to cosmic expanses.
