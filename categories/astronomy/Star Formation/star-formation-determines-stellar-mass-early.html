Star Formation Determines Stellar Mass Early

A star’s future is decided very quickly. Mass is mostly set during formation. After birth, growth nearly stops.

Most stellar mass is accumulated during the protostar phase. Once fusion ignites, strong radiation halts further infall. The surrounding gas is blown away. This locks in the star’s final size. Massive stars grow fast or not at all. Small differences early lead to huge differences later. Birth conditions dictate destiny.

Why This Matters

This matters because mass controls everything about a star. Lifespan, brightness, and death all depend on it. Star formation is the most important chapter.

It also explains stellar diversity. Stars differ because their births differed. Formation conditions echo across billions of years.

Did You Know?

A star’s mass can be decided in less than a million years.
