Stars Begin Life as Protostars

Stars spend millions of years as something else first. Before shining, they are dim, bloated, and unstable. Protostars are cosmic toddlers still figuring things out.

A protostar forms when collapsing gas becomes dense enough to trap heat. At this stage, nuclear fusion has not yet begun. The object glows faintly due to gravitational energy alone. Material from the surrounding disk continues falling onto the protostar, increasing its mass. Jets of gas often shoot out from its poles. These early years are messy and chaotic. Only later does fusion ignite and a true star is born.

Why This Matters

This matters because protostars reveal the earliest stages of stellar evolution. Studying them helps astronomers understand how stars gain mass. It also explains why stars don’t all form the same way.

It also helps explain planet formation. The disks around protostars eventually become planetary systems. Every planet begins life alongside a protostar.

Did You Know?

A protostar can be larger than the Sun but much cooler.
