🤯 Did You Know (click to read)

Grizzly bears time their hunts so their shadows blend with riverbank cover, surprising salmon.

Researchers discovered that grizzly bears often choose hunting times when low-angle sunlight casts shadows that merge with riverbank rocks, vegetation, or overhangs. This shadow timing allows bears to approach salmon without being detected. By aligning their position with environmental cover, they maximize stealth and strike success. Observations show bears adjusting posture and timing to ensure the shadow remains aligned as the sun moves. Juveniles learn by mimicking adults’ alignment strategies. The technique requires awareness of light direction, environmental features, and prey behavior. This behavior illustrates that grizzly predation incorporates both natural camouflage and precise temporal planning. Understanding shadow timing reveals an advanced interplay between light, landscape, and predator cognition.
