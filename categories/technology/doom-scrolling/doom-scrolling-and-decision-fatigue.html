Doom Scrolling and Decision Fatigue

Constant negative content leads to cognitive depletion and poor choices.

💥 Impact

Doom scrolling reduces mental energy for rational choices.

It demonstrates the far-reaching consequences of digital engagement.

🤯 Did You Know?

Doom scrolling induces decision fatigue, weakening judgment and self-control.

Each scroll presents multiple micro-decisions: click, read, share, comment. Over time, these decisions accumulate into cognitive fatigue, weakening executive function. Users may make impulsive or emotionally driven choices offline as a result. Studies indicate doom scrollers are more prone to overspending, unhealthy eating, or conflict in social interactions. Decision fatigue perpetuates the scrolling cycle: seeking reassurance or distraction prolongs engagement. The effect compounds with multitasking, further draining mental resources. Users may feel “mentally exhausted” even without physical effort. Interventions focus on structured information intake and decision automation. Understanding this helps explain why doom scrolling impacts not just mood but everyday life decisions.
