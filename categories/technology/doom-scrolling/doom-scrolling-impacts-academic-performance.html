Doom Scrolling Impacts Academic Performance

Students’ late-night scrolling affects focus, retention, and grades.

💥 Impact

Doom scrolling can undermine student learning and academic success.

It highlights a clear intersection between mental habits and educational outcomes.

🤯 Did You Know?

Doom scrolling late at night correlates with lower focus and academic performance.

Habitual doom scrolling reduces attention span and working memory, critical for learning. Sleep disruption further impairs cognitive function. Students report difficulty concentrating on assignments, slower reading comprehension, and decreased retention. Time spent scrolling often replaces study or homework periods. Studies correlate high doom scrolling frequency with lower GPA and higher stress levels. Academic interventions recommend screen-free study periods and scheduled social media breaks. Understanding doom scrolling’s impact helps educators design better digital well-being programs. Awareness campaigns encourage responsible media consumption to protect academic outcomes. The effect demonstrates that digital habits extend beyond leisure into measurable performance domains.
