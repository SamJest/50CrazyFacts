Early Blockchain-Free Smart Contracts?

Can smart contracts exist without blockchain? The answer is yes—and it happened first.

💥 Impact

Recognizing pre-blockchain smart contracts challenges the common misconception that Ethereum invented them. They existed as theoretical and practical constructs decades prior.

This perspective emphasizes that technological revolutions are iterative, building upon forgotten or overlooked foundations.

🤯 Did You Know?

Before blockchain, experimental smart contracts ran on centralized systems, demonstrating self-executing agreements as early as the 1990s.

In the mid-1990s, researchers built experimental smart contracts without any blockchain at all. They relied on centralized databases, cryptographic signatures, and automated triggers to execute agreements. One example involved an escrow system that released funds when conditions were digitally verified. Another was a primitive domain-name trading protocol that automatically processed transfers. These systems were limited—they lacked decentralization, scalability, or trustlessness—but the concept of a contract executing itself was already there. People marveled at the idea of code enforcing obligations without lawyers, showing that the “smart” part isn’t inherently tied to blockchain. It’s about automation, reliability, and enforceability. This history is often forgotten because blockchain stole the spotlight, but it reveals the layered evolution of the idea. It also demonstrates that innovation often begins with small, imperfect experiments before being transformed by disruptive infrastructure.
