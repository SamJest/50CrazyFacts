Early Phishing Kits: DIY Cybercrime in the ’90s

Before malware marketplaces, phishers made their own kits from scratch.

💥 Impact

These early phishing kits foreshadowed the commercialization of cybercrime, showing that technical skill isn’t the only barrier to entry.

By examining these kits, we understand that phishing has always been accessible to clever amateurs, not just sophisticated hackers.

🤯 Did You Know?

Early phishing kits in the 1990s allowed teenagers to automate scams on AOL with almost zero coding experience.

In the late 1990s, a curious phenomenon emerged: ready-made phishing kits. Unlike today, where complex software can be downloaded or bought on the dark web, early phishers cobbled together scripts, fake login pages, and email templates themselves. Teenagers with minimal programming knowledge were able to automate attacks on AOL and similar platforms. Some even shared these kits on hacker forums, effectively democratizing cybercrime before “democratizing” had a tech meaning. The kits were rudimentary, but they worked well enough to snare dozens of unsuspecting users in a single night. It’s almost absurd to imagine kids dialing up on modems, copying scripts, and turning them into small-scale cyber heists. These DIY tools highlight the ingenuity and audacity required to launch attacks before professionalized cybercrime emerged.
