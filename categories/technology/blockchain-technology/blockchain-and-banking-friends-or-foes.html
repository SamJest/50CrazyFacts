Blockchain and Banking: Friends or Foes?

The very system meant to bypass banks is being embraced by them—ironically.

💥 Impact

Blockchain adoption by banks reveals the pragmatism of financial institutions in integrating disruptive tech.

It also sparks debate about whether blockchain’s original philosophy is compromised when centralized entities dominate usage.

🤯 Did You Know?

Blockchain is now used by banks for settlements, despite originally aiming to bypass financial institutions.

Initially designed to sidestep financial institutions, blockchain has been rapidly adopted by banks and payment networks. JP Morgan created its own digital currency, JPM Coin, and numerous banks explore blockchain for cross-border settlements and fraud reduction. The irony is thick: a technology promising decentralization is being harnessed by the very central authorities it sought to circumvent. Banks benefit from faster settlements, reduced intermediaries, and improved transparency while retaining control over assets. Some blockchain platforms even integrate compliance protocols directly into the network, merging regulation with decentralized architecture. This creates a hybrid model where legacy finance meets cutting-edge cryptography, redefining trust. Critics argue this undermines blockchain’s ideological roots, while proponents see practical utility. The tension between ideals and adoption demonstrates how transformative technology evolves under social and economic pressures.
