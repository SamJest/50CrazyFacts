World’s Largest MR Installation

An entire city block transformed into MR—literally.

💥 Impact

Large-scale MR installations prove that perception can be extended beyond single users to entire communities, opening new frontiers in urban design and entertainment.

Massive MR projects reveal the interplay of technology, psychology, and social behavior at unprecedented scales.

🤯 Did You Know?

In Singapore, a whole city block was turned into MR, letting thousands interact with floating holograms.

In 2018, a team in Singapore created what is considered the world’s largest mixed reality installation, overlaying digital art and interactive objects across a full city block. Participants could navigate streets, parks, and storefronts while seeing holograms integrated seamlessly into urban architecture. The scale required dozens of synchronized MR devices, high-precision tracking, and real-time data streaming to thousands of users. Engineers faced extreme challenges: varying light conditions, unpredictable crowds, and interference from reflective surfaces threatened immersion at every step. The project was less about practicality and more about testing human perception at scale: could dozens of people simultaneously experience MR in a complex, uncontrolled environment? The answer was yes, though some participants reported dizziness and minor spatial confusion. This extreme deployment demonstrated that MR could go beyond personal or industrial use, operating in public, chaotic spaces. It also provided invaluable data for future urban MR, gamification, and public art projects. By pushing boundaries of scale, the installation challenged assumptions about MR’s spatial and social limits.
