The Forgotten Role of Illustrations

Could a picture change how you read a novel? Victorians knew it could.

💥 Impact

Illustrations amplified narrative, suspense, and reader interpretation, functioning as forgotten co-authors of Victorian stories.

Visual integration demonstrates the multimodal sophistication of Victorian literature, often overlooked today.

🤯 Did You Know?

Victorian novels used illustrations strategically to shape suspense, interpretation, and reader engagement.

Illustrations were central to many serialized novels, from Dickens’ *Pickwick Papers* to Collins’ works, but are now largely forgotten in literary discussions. Skilled illustrators like Hablot Knight Browne (Phiz) collaborated closely with authors to shape readers’ imagination, timing, and emotional response. Visual cues reinforced suspense, foreshadowing, or comedic moments. Readers often discussed illustrations as much as text, sometimes disagreeing with authorial intent. The integration of visual and textual storytelling created a multimedia experience ahead of its time. Illustrations also guided interpretation, particularly in Gothic or sensation fiction, emphasizing horror, mystery, or domestic drama. Over time, as editions became standardized, illustrations were minimized, and their influence faded from literary memory. Yet in their era, they functioned as narrative mechanics, shaping engagement, empathy, and suspense. Forgotten visual strategies reveal how Victorian storytelling operated across multiple sensory channels. This synergy of text and image contributed to immersive reading experiences that were extreme for their time.
