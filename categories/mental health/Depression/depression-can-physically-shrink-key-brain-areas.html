Depression Can Physically Shrink Key Brain Areas

Depression changes more than mood. It alters the brain. The effects can be measured.

Depression has been linked to reduced volume in the hippocampus, a brain region tied to memory and learning. Long-term depression increases cortisol, a stress hormone that can damage neurons. Brain imaging studies show measurable structural differences in people with chronic depression. These changes can affect memory recall, emotional regulation, and stress responses. The brain remains plastic, meaning treatment can reverse some effects. Therapy and medication support neurogenesis over time. This discovery reshaped how scientists view depression as a physical illness.

Why This Matters

It matters because depression is not imagined or exaggerated. Brain structure changes validate lived experiences. This reduces stigma and encourages medical treatment.

Understanding physical brain impact drives better research funding. It also improves public acceptance of depression as a medical condition.

Did You Know?

Did you know the hippocampus can regrow with effective treatment and reduced stress levels. This recovery has been observed in longitudinal brain scans.
