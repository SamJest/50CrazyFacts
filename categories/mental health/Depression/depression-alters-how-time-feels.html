Depression Alters How Time Feels

People with depression experience time differently. Minutes can feel endless. Days may blur together.

Depression disrupts the brain’s internal clock, altering time perception. Studies show depressed individuals often feel time moves more slowly. This is linked to reduced dopamine activity, which affects reward anticipation. When pleasure is muted, moments feel longer and heavier. This distortion worsens motivation and decision-making. It explains why simple tasks feel exhausting. Time perception often normalizes with symptom improvement.

Why This Matters

This matters because it explains functional impairment. Difficulty acting is not laziness. Time distortion compounds daily struggles.

Recognizing this helps clinicians tailor expectations and pacing in treatment plans. It also improves empathy from caregivers.

Did You Know?

Did you know altered time perception is used as a diagnostic clue in clinical interviews. It helps distinguish depression from anxiety disorders.
