Depression Can Alter Moral Risk-Taking

Rules feel heavier. Fear of mistakes grows. Choices shrink.

Depression changes how risk is evaluated. People become overly cautious with moral decisions. The brain exaggerates potential harm. Even neutral choices feel dangerous. This links to heightened guilt processing. Decision fatigue follows.

Why This Matters

It matters because over-caution limits life engagement. Opportunities are avoided unnecessarily.

Understanding this helps tailor therapy. Decision confidence can be rebuilt.

Did You Know?

Did you know risk aversion in depression has been measured using moral dilemma tasks. Results show consistent patterns.

Source

[Journal of Affective Disorders, elsevier.com]
