Depression Can Suppress Physical Pain

Some depressed people feel less pain. Others feel more. Both are linked to brain chemistry.

Depression alters pain processing pathways in the brain. Reduced serotonin and norepinephrine affect pain modulation. Some individuals report numbness or reduced pain sensitivity. Others experience amplified pain due to inflammation. This paradox complicates diagnosis and treatment. Chronic pain and depression frequently co-occur. Treating one often improves the other.

Why This Matters

This matters because pain symptoms may mask depression. Physical complaints deserve mental health screening.

It also affects medication choices, since antidepressants can treat pain disorders. Integrated care improves outcomes.

Did You Know?

Did you know some antidepressants are prescribed primarily for nerve pain. Their mood effects are secondary in those cases.
