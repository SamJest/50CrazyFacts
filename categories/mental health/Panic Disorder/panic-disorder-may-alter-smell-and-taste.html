Panic Disorder May Alter Smell and Taste

Scents feel amplified.

Panic attacks sometimes make ordinary smells intense or strange, as the brain overreacts to sensory input. Food may taste bitter or metallic, and perfumes may feel overwhelming. Neurological hyperarousal during attacks heightens senses, creating a temporary distortion. These sensory changes can increase anxiety, making recovery slower.

Why This Matters

Helps explain why sufferers avoid crowded places with strong scents.

It underscores the mind-body connection in panic disorders.

Did You Know?

Did you know smell distortions are temporary during panic episodes.
