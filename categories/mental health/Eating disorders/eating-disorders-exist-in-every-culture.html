Eating Disorders Exist in Every Culture

They are not a modern invention.

Eating disorders have been documented across cultures and centuries, not just in Western societies. Ancient Greek texts describe self-starvation, while medieval religious fasting sometimes mirrored anorexia nervosa. Modern research shows eating disorders appear globally, including rural and non-industrialized regions. Cultural expression varies, but the core psychological distress is consistent. This challenges the myth that eating disorders are purely caused by social media or Western beauty standards. Understanding this long history helps frame eating disorders as medical conditions, not trends.

Why This Matters

Recognizing the global and historical presence of eating disorders reduces stigma.

It encourages healthcare systems worldwide to treat them as serious mental health conditions rather than cultural anomalies.

Did You Know?

Did you know ancient Roman physician Galen wrote about pathological refusal of food nearly 2,000 years ago.
