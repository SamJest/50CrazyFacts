Eating Disorders Can Coexist with Autism

The overlap is stronger than expected.

Research shows higher rates of eating disorders among autistic individuals. Sensory sensitivities, rigidity, and anxiety around food play a role. Avoidant restrictive food intake disorder is especially common. Traditional treatments may need adaptation for neurodivergent patients. Personalized approaches improve outcomes. This intersection is gaining clinical attention.

Why This Matters

This matters for accurate diagnosis and care.

It encourages inclusive treatment models that respect neurodiversity.
