Baltimore in 1849 was notorious for election-related violence, including coercion, kidnapping, and repeat voting, known as 'cooping.'

Edgar Allan Poe passed away in early October 1849, mere days before a contentious Baltimore municipal election. The timing has led some historians to link his death to cooping—a practice of kidnapping and forcing individuals to vote multiple times for a candidate. Eyewitness reports of Poe’s wandering and strange attire support the possibility that he was a victim of such electoral manipulation. Political violence and voter fraud were common in Baltimore during this period, with gangs often exploiting vulnerable individuals. Although no concrete evidence ties Poe directly to cooping, the chronological coincidence fuels speculation. The scenario also aligns with urban crime patterns of the mid-19th century. Poe’s death, therefore, intersects with broader societal issues, including political corruption and the hazards of city life. The timing transforms a personal tragedy into a historical event with social implications. It suggests that even famous literary figures were not immune to systemic urban dangers.
