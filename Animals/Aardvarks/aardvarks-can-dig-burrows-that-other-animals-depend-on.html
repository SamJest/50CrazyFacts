Aardvarks Can Dig Burrows That Other Animals Depend On

Aardvarks don’t just build homes for themselves. They create real estate for entire ecosystems. Many animals move in after they leave.

Aardvark burrows can be several meters long and are dug using powerful claws and shoulders. Once abandoned, these burrows are used by dozens of other species. Hyenas, warthogs, porcupines, reptiles, and birds all shelter inside. The burrows provide protection from heat, predators, and fires. Aardvarks unintentionally act as ecosystem engineers.

Why This Matters

It matters because their digging supports biodiversity. Removing aardvarks affects many other animals.

This shows how one species can shape an environment without meaning to. Aardvarks quietly hold ecosystems together.

Did You Know?

In some regions, over 20 different species have been recorded using a single aardvark burrow. Many cannot dig their own shelters.
