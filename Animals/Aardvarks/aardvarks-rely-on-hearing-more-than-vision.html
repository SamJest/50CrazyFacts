Aardvarks Rely on Hearing More Than Vision

To an aardvark, the night is loud even when it looks empty. Sound guides almost every decision. Eyes are secondary tools.

Aardvarks have relatively poor eyesight but extremely sensitive hearing. Their long ears can rotate independently to pinpoint sounds. This helps them detect predators like lions or hyenas long before visual contact. Hearing is also crucial when insects move inside mounds. In darkness, sound becomes their primary sense.

Why This Matters

It matters because it explains why aardvarks thrive at night. They are adapted to an auditory world humans barely notice.

It also shows how different sensory priorities can shape behavior. Survival doesn’t always depend on sight.

Did You Know?

Aardvarks can hear termites moving inside walls of soil. This allows them to choose the most active feeding sites.
