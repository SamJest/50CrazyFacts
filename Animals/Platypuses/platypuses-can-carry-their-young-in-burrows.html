Platypuses Can Carry Their Young in Burrows

Careful parenting.

Female platypuses build burrows with nesting chambers for their eggs. After hatching, young remain in burrows for about three to four months. Mothers curl around hatchlings to provide warmth and protection. Young feed on milk secreted onto the skin and fur, as platypuses lack nipples. Burrows are dug into riverbanks and can be extensive, with multiple entrances. The structure safeguards against flooding and predators. Parenting is solitary but highly protective.
