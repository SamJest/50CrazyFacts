Platypuses Are Mammals That Lay Eggs

Egg-laying mammals?

Unlike most mammals, female platypuses lay 1–3 eggs per clutch. Eggs are incubated by curling around them for about 10 days. Hatchlings emerge tiny, blind, and hairless. Despite laying eggs, mothers nurse their young with milk secreted from mammary glands. This combination of reptilian egg-laying and mammalian nursing is unique. The practice ensures young receive vital nutrients and protection in early life. Egg-laying also reduces predation risks compared to open nests.

Why This Matters

It highlights a rare reproductive strategy in mammals.

This egg-laying ability provides insight into early mammalian evolution.

Did You Know?

Hatchlings feed by licking milk from the mother’s fur since platypuses lack nipples.
