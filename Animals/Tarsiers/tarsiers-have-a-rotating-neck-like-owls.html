Tarsiers Have a Rotating Neck Like Owls

360-degree awareness.

Tarsiers can rotate their heads almost 180 degrees in each direction, like owls. Fixed eyes force them to use neck rotation for visual scanning. This allows them to remain motionless while surveying their surroundings. The adaptation is crucial for spotting prey and predators in dense forests. Neck flexibility requires specialized vertebrae and musculature. It supports precise, energy-efficient hunting. Such rotation improves survival without revealing position. Tarsiers’ rotating necks exemplify anatomical specialization for nocturnal predation.

Why This Matters

Neck rotation compensates for immobile eyes.

It enables stealthy hunting and predator vigilance.
