Tarsiers’ Eyes Are Immobile But Exceptionally Large

Fixed and focused.

Tarsiers’ eyes are large relative to their skulls and cannot move in their sockets. Each eye is roughly the size of the brain, allowing maximum light capture at night. Immobility requires head rotation to scan surroundings. Large eyes enhance visual acuity in low-light hunting conditions. Eye anatomy supports nocturnal specialization and precise predation. Evolution prioritizes sensitivity over mobility for energy-efficient vision. Tarsiers’ eye structure is a unique adaptation among primates. Large, immobile eyes complement other nocturnal adaptations like ultrasonic hearing.

Why This Matters

Immense eyes improve night vision.

They require head rotation, supporting stealth and precise hunting.

Did You Know?

Tarsiers have large, immobile eyes optimized for nocturnal vision.
