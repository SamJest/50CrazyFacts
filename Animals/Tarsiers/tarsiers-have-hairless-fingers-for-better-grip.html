Tarsiers Have Hairless Fingers for Better Grip

Grip perfection.

Tarsiers’ fingertips are partially hairless, enhancing tactile sensation and grip. This allows precise handling of branches, prey, and delicate movements during leaps. Hairless pads increase friction, improving stability on smooth or wet surfaces. They also help in manipulating insects and small lizards while hunting. Tactile sensitivity compensates for limited hand-eye coordination in low-light conditions. Finger adaptations demonstrate the intricate link between morphology and arboreal survival. Tarsiers’ hands exemplify specialized evolution for climbing and hunting.
