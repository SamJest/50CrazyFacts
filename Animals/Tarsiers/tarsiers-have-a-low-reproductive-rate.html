Tarsiers Have a Low Reproductive Rate

Slow breeders.

Tarsiers produce a single offspring per year or every two years. Gestation lasts about six months, and infants cling to the mother after birth. Low reproductive output makes populations vulnerable to habitat loss and predation. Parental investment is high, with extended care for survival skills. Conservation efforts are challenged by limited reproductive rates. Slow breeding reflects evolutionary trade-offs for small, specialized primates. Population recovery after disturbances is naturally slow. Tarsiers’ reproductive biology informs captive breeding programs and conservation strategies.
