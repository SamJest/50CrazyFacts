Axolotls Can Regrow Lungs if Damaged

Not just limbs or hearts—axolotls can repair their lungs too, restoring full respiratory function.

When lung tissue is injured, axolotls mobilize specialized cells to rebuild alveolar structures without scarring. This allows them to resume normal breathing and survive in variable aquatic conditions. The regeneration involves cellular dedifferentiation, proliferation, and precise tissue patterning. Unlike mammals, scar tissue formation is avoided, making regrowth highly effective. This ability is vital in low-oxygen waters or after trauma. Scientists study axolotls to understand lung repair, which could one day inform human therapies. Lung regeneration adds another layer to their already remarkable regenerative repertoire.

Why This Matters

Shows vertebrates can recover from organ damage previously thought irreversible.

It offers models for medical research into pulmonary injury and regenerative medicine.

Did You Know?

Lungs can regenerate in a matter of weeks with complete function restored.
