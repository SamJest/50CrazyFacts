Axolotls Can Regenerate Spinal Cord Neurons Multiple Times

Not only can axolotls repair their spinal cords, they can do it repeatedly without losing function.

This extraordinary ability involves neurons dedifferentiating and regrowing after injury, reconnecting perfectly every time. Unlike humans, axolotls avoid scar tissue formation that would block nerve reconnection. Experiments show that even after multiple spinal injuries, axolotls regain full swimming and reflex abilities. This resilience is due to specialized stem-cell-like cells that orchestrate regrowth and precise tissue patterning. Their repeated neuronal regeneration is unparalleled among vertebrates. Studying these mechanisms offers hope for future treatments for human spinal injuries and neurodegenerative diseases. It also fascinates neuroscientists looking for models of perfect nerve repair.

Why This Matters

Repeated spinal regeneration provides a living model for how vertebrates can overcome nerve damage without permanent deficits.

It underscores the axolotl’s value in scientific research and highlights nature’s ingenuity in solving complex biological problems.

Did You Know?

They can recover from multiple spinal injuries over their lifetime without lasting impairment.
