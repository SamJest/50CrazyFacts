Axolotls Can Detect Predators Using Vibration Patterns

They might seem defenseless, but axolotls are sensitive to subtle threats in their environment.

Using lateral lines, axolotls detect predator movements as vibrations in water. This system allows them to flee before visual detection is possible. Even subtle pressure waves from approaching predators trigger escape responses. It complements their other senses, including skin photoreception and chemical detection. This sophisticated sensory integration increases survival chances in murky or low-light waters. It’s an evolutionary advantage that supports their continued survival despite urban and natural threats.
