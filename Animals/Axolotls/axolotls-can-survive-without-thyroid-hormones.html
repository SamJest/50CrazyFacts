Axolotls Can Survive Without Thyroid Hormones

While most amphibians need thyroid hormones to mature, axolotls defy the rule.

Axolotls’ neoteny is partly due to inactive thyroid signaling. They can reach sexual maturity without ever undergoing metamorphosis, retaining larval features like gills and aquatic skin. This allows them to remain aquatic and avoid terrestrial risks. In lab studies, artificially increasing thyroid hormones forces metamorphosis, but naturally, they thrive without it. This unique endocrine setup has intrigued biologists for decades. It’s one reason axolotls are used to study growth, hormone regulation, and developmental biology. Their ability to bypass metamorphosis illustrates a rare evolutionary shortcut in vertebrates. Retaining juvenile features for life gives them ecological advantages in their native lakes.
