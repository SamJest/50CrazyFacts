Axolotls Can Live in Water Polluted With Heavy Metals

If survival in harsh water was a contest, axolotls would win. They show remarkable resilience against toxins.

Studies have shown that axolotls can survive in water with low levels of heavy metals like lead or cadmium for short periods. They can detoxify or tolerate pollutants better than many amphibians, though prolonged exposure is harmful. Their ability to endure temporary pollution is aided by specialized detoxification enzymes and adaptive kidney function. In the wild, urban lakes around Mexico City have pushed them to tolerate suboptimal conditions. This resilience has made them important models for environmental toxicology research. Researchers use them to study how pollution impacts aquatic ecosystems and regeneration under stress. Their survival in moderately polluted water underscores both their hardiness and the urgency of habitat protection.
