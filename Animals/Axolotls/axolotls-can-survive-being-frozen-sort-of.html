Axolotls Can Survive Being Frozen—Sort Of!

While most animals would die in icy waters, axolotls have a peculiar tolerance to cold that defies expectation.

Axolotls can survive near-freezing temperatures for short periods by slowing down metabolism dramatically. They enter a kind of torpor, reducing oxygen needs and heart rate to almost nothing. This survival trick allows them to withstand sudden winter drops in temperature, especially in high-altitude lakes in Mexico. However, prolonged freezing will still kill them, and they cannot survive actual ice encasement like some frogs. Researchers study their cold tolerance to understand metabolic slowdown and potential medical applications, like preserving organs for transplant. The process is reversible; once temperatures rise, the axolotl wakes up and resumes normal activity. It’s a tiny but impressive adaptation to a harsh environment.

Why This Matters

Their cold tolerance shows how animals can survive in extreme and fluctuating climates.

It also suggests possible biomedical applications for hypothermia treatment or organ preservation.

Did You Know?

They can survive for hours at 2°C without food or oxygen before resuming normal activity.
