Axolotls Can Regrow Eyes if Removed

Even complex organs like eyes aren’t safe from their regenerative powers.

If an axolotl loses an eye, it can regrow a fully functional one, complete with retina, lens, and optic nerves. This involves specialized progenitor cells forming a blastema at the site of removal, then differentiating into all necessary ocular tissues. Vision is restored without scarring or misalignment. This is unique among vertebrates, where eye regeneration is extremely limited. The process provides scientists with a living model to study eye development, injury repair, and potential therapies for blindness. Watching an eye reform is both eerie and awe-inspiring. Eye regeneration complements limb, heart, and spinal cord repair capabilities.
