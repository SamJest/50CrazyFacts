Axolotls Can Survive Partial Brain Injury

Even severe head injuries don’t always spell doom for axolotls.

Axolotls can regenerate portions of their brain after trauma, restoring lost neurons and function. This involves dedifferentiation of nearby cells and guided regrowth of neural networks. Cognitive and sensory functions are largely preserved after regeneration. Researchers study axolotls to understand how vertebrates can repair central nervous tissue. This has huge implications for human neurobiology and treatments for brain injury. The process is remarkably precise, avoiding scar tissue that would otherwise inhibit regrowth.
