Lemurs Communicate with Unique Scent Signals

Nose knows best.

Lemurs have specialized scent glands on their wrists and chest to mark territory and communicate reproductive status. Troop members interpret these chemical signals to establish hierarchy and find mates.

AD 1

Why This Matters

Advanced chemical communication reduces conflict and enhances reproductive success.

It demonstrates the power of non-vocal communication in animal societies.

Did You Know?

Male ring-tailed lemurs perform “stink fights” by rubbing tails with scent before waving them at rivals.
