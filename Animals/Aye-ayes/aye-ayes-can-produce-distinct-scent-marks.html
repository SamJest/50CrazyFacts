Aye-Ayes Can Produce Distinct Scent Marks

They communicate silently using chemical signals.

Aye-ayes mark branches and tree trunks with secretions from glands near the mouth or genitals. These scent marks convey information about territory, reproductive status, and individual identity. Other aye-ayes can detect these marks days later, guiding social interactions without direct contact. This non-vocal communication reduces conflict in overlapping ranges and enhances reproductive success. Scent marking is crucial for solitary nocturnal animals.
