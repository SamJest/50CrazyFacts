Aye-Ayes Have Flexible Joints That Aid in Crawling

Their skeletal system supports unusual movement.

Aye-ayes have highly flexible shoulder, wrist, and ankle joints that allow twisting and turning in tight spaces. This flexibility is crucial for moving through dense vegetation and accessing difficult-foraging sites. Combined with strong fingers and claws, they can climb upside down, hang, and navigate complex branch networks. Joint mobility also reduces the risk of injury during acrobatics. It’s an essential part of their survival strategy in arboreal environments.
