Aye-Ayes Can Sense Insect Movements Beneath Bark

They are tiny prey detectives.

Their specialized finger and acute hearing allow aye-ayes to detect the faintest movements of larvae inside wood. By tapping and listening carefully, they pinpoint cavities before gnawing. This combination of touch, hearing, and smell makes them extremely efficient foragers. Even slight vibrations from larvae can be detected from several centimeters inside a tree. Such sensory integration is rare among primates. This adaptation ensures they consistently access high-protein food with minimal wasted effort.
