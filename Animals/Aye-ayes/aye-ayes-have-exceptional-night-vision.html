Aye-Ayes Have Exceptional Night Vision

These primates’ eyes are like built-in night goggles.

Aye-ayes have enormous, forward-facing eyes with a high density of rod cells, allowing them to see in near-total darkness. This adaptation is crucial for nocturnal foraging and avoiding predators. Their night vision works in tandem with acute hearing and percussive foraging, making them highly effective nighttime hunters. Unlike diurnal primates, their visual system prioritizes sensitivity over color discrimination. The large corneas and pupils maximize light intake, giving them an edge in low-light forest environments. This combination of features allows them to navigate safely and efficiently after dark.
