Aye-Ayes Can Learn from Experience

They are surprisingly intelligent for nocturnal primates.

Aye-ayes demonstrate problem-solving skills and can remember successful techniques for extracting prey. Individuals can modify tapping patterns based on previous success or difficulty. This learning ability enhances foraging efficiency and reduces wasted energy. Behavioral plasticity also helps them navigate human-altered environments or avoid new predators. Such cognitive traits are rare among solitary nocturnal primates. Their intelligence is closely tied to their physical adaptations, especially finger dexterity and sensory acuity.
