Scent as Primary Communication

Method of scent marking allows aye-ayes to communicate territory.

They mark trees and branches, signaling presence without confrontation.

Why This Matters

This strategy replaces vocal communication in a nocturnal environment.

Scent marking helps maintain social spacing and reduce conflicts.

Did You Know?

Did you know a single scent mark can persist for days, communicating presence long after the animal leaves?
