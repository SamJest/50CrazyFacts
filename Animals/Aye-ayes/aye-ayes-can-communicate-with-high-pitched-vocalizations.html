Aye-Ayes Can Communicate With High-Pitched Vocalizations

They aren’t silent creatures of the night.

Aye-ayes produce a variety of squeaks, clicks, and screeches to communicate with other aye-ayes. Calls serve purposes such as signaling territory, warning of predators, or coordinating mating. The high frequency ensures sounds travel effectively through dense forest foliage. Vocal communication complements chemical and visual signals, creating a multi-channel social system. Researchers study these calls to understand social structure and stress levels. Despite being largely solitary, vocalizations help maintain awareness of nearby conspecifics.
