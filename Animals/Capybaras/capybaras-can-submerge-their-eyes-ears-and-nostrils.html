Capybaras Can Submerge Their Eyes, Ears, and Nostrils

They’re stealthy swimmers.

When hiding from predators or cooling off, capybaras can submerge almost their entire head while keeping eyes, ears, and nostrils aligned to see, hear, and breathe. This unique adaptation allows them to remain almost invisible in water. Juveniles learn this technique from adults. Predators such as jaguars or caimans often overlook a camouflaged capybara. Submersion also aids in thermoregulation and parasite avoidance.

Why This Matters

Shows sophisticated survival adaptations.

It highlights evolutionary innovation in semi-aquatic rodents.

Did You Know?

They can stay almost fully submerged with minimal exposure above water for extended periods.
