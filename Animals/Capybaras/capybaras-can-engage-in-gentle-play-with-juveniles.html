Capybaras Can Engage in Gentle Play With Juveniles

Play supports growth.

Adults often encourage playful interactions with juveniles, including chasing, mock biting, and splashing. Play develops motor skills, teaches social rules, and strengthens bonds. Juveniles learn swimming, diving, and group behavior through play. Such interactions are critical for survival skills and group cohesion. Play behavior persists into adulthood in some contexts.

Why This Matters

Shows cognitive, social, and physical development through play.

It highlights the adaptive importance of fun in animal learning.

Did You Know?

They may splash, chase, or gently nip juveniles during play to teach social skills.
