Capybaras Can Live in Groups of Over 100

They thrive in crowds.

While typical groups range from 10–20, capybaras sometimes form “supergroups” of over 100 individuals near abundant water and food sources. These massive aggregations improve predator detection and provide social stability. Within these groups, multiple dominant males may oversee different subgroups, maintaining order. Large gatherings also allow for shared vigilance, coordinated movements, and efficient mating. Group living reduces individual predation risk through safety in numbers. Such social flexibility helps capybaras adapt to environmental pressures.

Why This Matters

Shows extreme social adaptability in rodents.

It highlights the benefits of collective vigilance and coordination.

Did You Know?

In exceptional cases, hundreds of capybaras can be observed grazing together in floodplain habitats.
