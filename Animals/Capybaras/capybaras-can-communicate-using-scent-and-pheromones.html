Capybaras Can Communicate Using Scent and Pheromones

Smell is a language of its own.

Males and females use scent glands on their noses and anal regions to communicate reproductive status, territory, and dominance. Females can detect ovulating males and respond accordingly. Scent marking reduces conflicts by signaling intentions without physical confrontation. This form of communication complements vocalizations and visual signals. Juveniles learn to recognize these chemical cues early. Understanding these signals is key to interpreting group dynamics.
