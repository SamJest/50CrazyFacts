Capybaras Can Use Water to Avoid Insect Pests

Immersion as relief.

By entering water, capybaras reduce exposure to biting insects like mosquitoes and flies. Partial submersion cools the skin, flushes away pests, and lowers irritation. Group submersion amplifies this effect. Such behavior demonstrates an adaptive strategy to mitigate environmental stressors. In wetland ecosystems, this is crucial for health and comfort.

Why This Matters

Shows behavioral adaptation to parasites and environmental discomfort.

It highlights the interplay between habitat use and self-care strategies.

Did You Know?

They often remain partially submerged during peak insect activity to avoid bites.
