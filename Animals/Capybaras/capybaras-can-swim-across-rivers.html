Capybaras Can Swim Across Rivers

They’re natural river navigators.

Capybaras frequently swim across rivers or streams to forage or evade predators. Their buoyant bodies, webbed feet, and powerful strokes allow efficient long-distance swimming. Crossing waterways increases access to food and shelter. Juveniles learn navigation techniques from older capybaras. This behavior demonstrates their semi-aquatic lifestyle and adaptation to riparian habitats. Rivers also provide protection from terrestrial predators.
