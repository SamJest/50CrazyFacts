Sloths Can Turn Their Heads Almost Completely Around

Surveillance without moving.

Three-toed sloths have nine neck vertebrae, letting them rotate their heads up to 270 degrees. This extreme flexibility compensates for their slow body movements. They can scan for predators or locate food without leaving their perch. Two-toed sloths have slightly less rotation but still significant. This adaptation reduces energy expenditure while increasing situational awareness. It is unique among mammals, allowing sloths to remain almost invisible to predators. Neck flexibility is critical in dense rainforest foliage.

Why This Matters

Neck rotation enhances predator detection without movement.

It allows efficient feeding and vigilance.

Did You Know?

Three-toed sloths can practically look behind themselves without moving their bodies.
