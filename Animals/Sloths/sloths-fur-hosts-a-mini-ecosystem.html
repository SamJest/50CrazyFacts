Sloths’ Fur Hosts a Mini-Ecosystem

More than just hair.

Sloth fur is home to algae, fungi, and tiny insects, creating a micro-ecosystem. This includes sloth moths, which lay eggs in feces, completing their life cycle. Algae provides camouflage, while insects feed on fur and debris. This mutualistic relationship benefits both sloths and other species. The ecosystem is unique to each sloth and varies by region. It also promotes rainforest biodiversity. Sloths effectively carry their own tiny rainforest communities wherever they go.

Why This Matters

Hosting mini-ecosystems supports biodiversity.

It enhances sloth camouflage and survival.

Did You Know?

Sloth fur can support a complete tiny ecosystem, from algae to insects.
