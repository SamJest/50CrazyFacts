Sloths’ Curved Claws Are Multi-Purpose

Tree tools.

Sloths’ long, curved claws serve multiple purposes beyond hanging. They allow climbing, digging shallow holes for defecation, and even defending against predators. The claws also help maintain stability in high winds or storms. Claw shape differs slightly between two-toed and three-toed sloths, reflecting evolutionary adaptation. They rarely touch the ground directly, preserving claw sharpness. These claws are essential for their entire arboreal lifestyle. The strength and shape make the sloth one of the most secure tree-dwellers in the rainforest.

Why This Matters

Claws are vital for survival and feeding.

They prevent falls and allow safe arboreal life.

Did You Know?

Sloths’ claws are strong enough to grip firmly, dig, and defend, all while hanging upside down.
