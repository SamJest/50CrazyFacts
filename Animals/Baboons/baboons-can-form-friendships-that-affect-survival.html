Baboons Can Form Friendships That Affect Survival

They aren’t just rivals—they are friends.

Baboons form long-term social bonds through grooming, play, and support during conflicts. Strong friendships improve access to food, protection, and mating opportunities. Friends often cooperate in fights or share vigilance duties. Social bonds can influence stress levels and overall health. These relationships show that cooperative strategies are as important as dominance in survival.

Why This Matters

Highlights the adaptive value of friendship in primates.

It emphasizes social networks’ role in fitness and longevity.

Did You Know?

They maintain lifelong bonds with certain troop members, even across generations.
