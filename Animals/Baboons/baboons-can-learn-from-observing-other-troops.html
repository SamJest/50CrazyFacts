Baboons Can Learn From Observing Other Troops

They are keen social learners.

Baboons watch neighboring troops to learn about food sources, predator behavior, and new strategies. Observational learning allows rapid adaptation without trial-and-error. This reduces risk and energy expenditure. Troops may adopt successful behaviors seen in others, such as using new sleeping sites or accessing novel foods. Learning from others demonstrates intelligence and cultural transmission potential.
