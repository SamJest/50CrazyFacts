Baboons Can Exhibit Facial Expressions to Signal Submission

They know when to back down.

Subordinate baboons often bare their teeth, yawn, or flatten ears to signal submission. These expressions reduce aggression from dominant individuals and prevent escalation. Facial signaling is coupled with body posture and vocalizations for clarity. Subtle expressions can prevent conflict and maintain troop harmony. Understanding these signals is vital for navigating social hierarchies.

Why This Matters

Shows non-verbal communication as a conflict management tool.

It highlights social intelligence and emotional awareness in primates.

Did You Know?

They may smile with bared teeth as a peaceful gesture to higher-ranking individuals.
