Baboons Can Exhibit Sex-Specific Roles in Troop Defense

Males and females contribute differently.

Male baboons often lead confrontations with predators or rival troops, using size and strength. Females contribute by organizing the group, maintaining vigilance, and protecting offspring. Both sexes assess threats and respond strategically. These differentiated roles enhance overall troop survival. Understanding sex-specific behaviors reveals adaptive strategies in complex social systems. Roles may shift depending on individual age, experience, and environmental pressures.
