Crows Can Teach Each Other New Skills

Feathered mentors.

Adult crows actively demonstrate new behaviors, such as tool use or food retrieval, to younger crows. This teaching ensures the survival and skill development of the next generation. Juveniles learn by observing and mimicking adults, accelerating their cognitive growth. Teaching behavior shows planning, social awareness, and cultural transmission.

Why This Matters

Crows’ teaching demonstrates advanced social intelligence and knowledge transfer.

It highlights the role of mentorship in animal learning and survival.

Did You Know?

They may show a juvenile how to drop stones into water to retrieve floating food.
