Crows Can Remember Individual Crow Enemies

Grudges within their own species.

Crows remember which fellow crows have threatened, bullied, or stolen from them. They may avoid these individuals, call warnings to others, or retaliate later. Social memory is crucial for navigating complex hierarchies. Juveniles observe adult interactions to learn who is safe or dangerous. This demonstrates sophisticated social intelligence and long-term memory.

Why This Matters

Crows’ memory of peers highlights advanced social cognition.

It emphasizes learning, social awareness, and survival in complex flocks.

Did You Know?

They may refuse to share food or join a flock with a known aggressor.
