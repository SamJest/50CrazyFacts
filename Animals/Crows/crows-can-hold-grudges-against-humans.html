Crows Can Hold Grudges Against Humans

Memory with attitude.

Crows remember humans who have threatened them and may harass or avoid them in the future. They may alert other crows to the danger posed by a specific person. This grudge-holding demonstrates long-term memory, social communication, and learned behavior. Juveniles learn caution from adults. Avoiding or retaliating against threats improves survival.

Why This Matters

Crows’ ability to hold grudges shows social awareness and memory.

It highlights emotional and cognitive complexity in birds.

Did You Know?

They sometimes dive-bomb humans who previously threatened them.
